## Page 1

Objective: Combine training initiatives and methodologies with cognitive and behavioral DATA to optimize training effectiveness In every training population, there are hard-wired differences in the strengths and abilities of each participant. This is the primary reason that the results of training programs are so varied despite the content and training methodologies with one class to essentially be the same.

Looking at the normal distribution of the population reveals that the most

important differences are found in the ends of the bell curve.

•25% of the people learn quickly; understand complex relationships; can

solve most sales problems on the spot; can create innovative solutions.

•25% of the people can easily adapt to new possibilities; produce

extremely creative ideas; think well outside the box

•25% of the people can persuade other people to make buying

decisions; can control a sales process; can handle stalls and objections

•25% of the people naturally network and prospect; deliver enthusiastic

and energetic presentations

Participants like these blaze through sales-oriented training programs like

7-footers in a slam dunk contest. The degree to which the participants do

not share these strengths and abilities is the degree to which the training

program will be less successful.

Two truths exist: the client wants to train its people and they want to be

trained. It is also true that sales organizations are usually made up of teams

with different people playing different roles. These roles usually require

different sets of strengths and abilities.

## Page 2

•25% of the people enjoy working with detailed information; are careful

planners; organize things efficiently

•25% of the people follow procedures consistently; do not make

exceptions; comply with standards

•25% of the people can work alone or with little interaction with others

•25% of the people are extremely service-oriented; enjoy helping clients

•25% of the people work well when given good instructions

•25% of the people handle routine tasks well, not ever becoming bored

A training organization that can enable its clients to identify the best roles

for its employees and then train them in those roles will be exceptional

and offer a compelling difference.

Example: The Competitive Selling model is made up of elements that

depend upon hard-wired personality traits and cognitive abilities.

•Pipeline Management - The 25% of the world that cannot work well

with details and that is not organized struggles with this.

•Prospecting & Personal Marketing - 50% of the population loses

energy when interacting with other people.

•Great First Meetings - 25% arrive with little preparation. 25% are not

immediately engaging because of low personal energy.

•Objection Handling - 50% of the people cannot handle objections.

•Presenting - 50% of the people do not present effectively even when

given the script, the slides and the training.

•Negotiating - 50% of the people do not negotiate effectively.

•Closing - 50% of the people cannot close a sale.

•2nd Level Questioning - 50% of the people will not push questioning

to that level. 50% cannot see the path to that level.

## Page 3

•Sales Improv - 60% of the population cannot improv in the classic

sense.

When these people are identified, other options become obvious. In some

situations, they can be trained differently to be adequate at the sales role.

Imagine that the first step with a client is to “inventory” the strengths and

abilities of the sales team. Now the information is available to make a

myriad of decisions. The trainers are now able to be proactive, identifying

potential issues before starting. A different plan can be presented to the

client which optimizes their talent. The trainers become far more than a

service, and gain the role of advisors.

In a competitive market, they can be the company that distinguishes itself

by looking at the talent first before presenting a solution off the shelf.
