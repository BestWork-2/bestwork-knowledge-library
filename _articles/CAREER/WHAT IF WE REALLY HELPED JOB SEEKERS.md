## Page 1

What If We Really Helped Job Seekers Find Jobs They Can Do…

I have spoken before thousands of job seekers over the past few years as the

guest of wonderful programs that sincerely sought to help them. I have met

hundreds of passionate volunteers devoting their time to that same objective. In

the beginning, I was deeply moved by the plight of talented people who might be

good employees in the right job, but who were poor job seekers. They did not

interview well. They did not network well. They were often seeking the recapture

the same type of job which was lost, even though it was a category of job that no longer existed. They were being offered workshops and advice on improving their resumés. They were being given motivational speeches. They were being fed an endless diet of clever interviewing tips, networking tips, dressing for success tips and more and more tips. Career counselors and recruiters pitched a bagful of products and services. Outdated and ineffective assessment tools were marketed at “special prices” when packaged with a host of other career tools.

State governments followed a similar path of disconnection with what was really

happening in the job market.

At this point, it is important for me to explain my unique perspective. For over 25 years, I have been a leading authority on the business applications of psychological testing. It has been my privilege to work with several of the top people in that field and to review that industry and its many products. My firm is a thought leader in using DATA to acquire and manage talent, through understanding each individual’s unique set of strengths and abilities. My original experience with job seekers inspired me to design and develop a special application to actually help job seekers with their job search. The recommendations and coaching were specifically directed at the individual, based on their hard-wired traits and abilities. They were not some generalized comments that were meant to apply to whoever read them.

Another change of perspective was that of identifying poor job fits rather than

trying to suggest the perfect one. People have an infinite array of jobs they can do. There are a finite number of jobs that they really cannot do. It seemed to be more practical to identify those, leaving everything else as being OK to pursue.
