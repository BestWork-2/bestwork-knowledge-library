Job Ideas & Job Behaviors

I recall a conversation with a young woman working in an office

administrative position in a medium-sized printing company. She had

asked her boss about transferring to a sales position. She was

enthusiastic about the change and wonderfully optimistic. The owner of

the business affirmed her success in the admin job, but asked if I would

speak to her to be sure of the move into sales.

My first question was “Why do you want to be in sales?”

“Well,” she said, “I watch the salespeople leaving the office every

day to visit customers. I hate sitting at a desk all day. It would be

great to get out and go places. …and a lot of the time, they’re

taking customers out to lunch, and that would be fun.”

I pointed out that most of their time out of the office was spent cold

calling on businesses to find sales prospects. When they were with

customers, they were working to persuade them to buy additional

printing services.

“I don’t want to do either of those things!” she said. “That’s not the

idea I had in mind.”

The ideas that people have about jobs may be positive or negative, but

rarely do they include the actual job behaviors that are required to succeed

in that particular job. Job behaviors are the separate kinds of actions or

thinking that make up the job or what a person must do in that job. It is

important to unbundle the job behaviors from the idea of the job in order

to truly understand if that is a good career choice. Many people like the

idea of the job of an NFL quarterback, imagining the thrill of hoisting the

Super Bowl Trophy after a winning season. The job behaviors that are not

so appealing are being hit by 300 lb. tackles and bouncing off the icy field

in Foxboro. The people who can handle those job behaviors are far fewer.

This is especially important for students who are considering possible

career choices. Few students have any depth of knowledge or experience

about many, if any, jobs. Their ideas may have come from television,

movies, their favorite class in high school, literature, advice from family or

friends or a random life event: -Physics is my favorite class and Mr. Becket is a fantastic teacher.

-The forensics science on NCIS is fascinating.

-Lawyers have engaging cases with interesting people, make a lot of

money and are respected by everyone.

-Doctors help people and live exciting lives.

-Engineers always have jobs.

-Stockbrokers work on Wall Street and are rich.

-Teaching is fun and rewarding.

Whatever the source, there is far more to the story. These may be good

choices. They may also be bad choices, or just OK choices. It is not

possible to say that one type of job is the best choice, or even a good one.

It is possible to identify the ones that are poor choices.

All jobs consist of sets of job behaviors, the individual elements of the job.

All job behaviors depend upon hard-wired personality traits and cognitive

abilities in order to be performed successfully. If a person lacks the

necessary traits or abilities, it is unlikely they can perform the job well. In

fact, without the critical traits and abilities, it will require extraordinary

effort and concentration just to achieve adequate performance. It is

virtually impossible to ever excel in such a role.

There are an infinite number of jobs that will ultimately be good or at least

OK for every student. There are only a finite number of jobs that are poor

fits for any individual student’s strengths and abilities. Today Celebrate

Strengths has easy to use tools that measure those strengths and abilities

and relate the results to hundreds of kinds of jobs. This amazing
