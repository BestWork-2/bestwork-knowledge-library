Job Agility in a Time of Change

There is no shortage of challenges facing the global workforce…economic

challenges, climate change conflicts, the advent of AI and more. The epicenter of these forces is always people. It is people who must deal with surviving these changes. When all of the rhetoric is finished and the same inadequate proposals have been proposed once more, it all comes down to an individual worker and the job they need. It is no longer possible to rely on long term job stability. In a time of change, job agility is what matters.

What is job agility? The Oxford Dictionary offers two definitions : •the ability to move quickly and easily •the ability to think and understand quickly.

If an individual learns quickly and adapts to changes easily, they can be described as having agility in that they are able to quickly learn the knowledge and skills of a new job and deploy them with little reference to how they were done in previous jobs.

In the context of this paper, I am speaking about the ability to identify an

individual’s capabilities in a variety of jobs using the DATA on their hard-wired personality traits and cognitive abilities. All jobs consist of a set of job behaviors, which are effectively rented by the employer. These can be easily matched to the behavioral strengths of an individual, and this matching is independent of any reference to skills or job knowledge. Since most recruiters do not know the behavioral strengths of candidates, they are forced to fall back on skills and experience or diplomas. If a candidate does know their behavioral strengths, they can document their expected success in a new job, providing information far more reliable than a resumé.

Interests, Motivation and Values are all part of what you want to do. Skills,

Experience and Education are what you know and have done. The personality

traits and cognitive abilities are what you can do, and this is the heart of job

agility.

Until now, the key to that job agility was missing. Without reliable metrics on job performance that linked individuals with jobs that fit their hard-wired capabilities, the perennial offerings of re-skilling and innovation were trotted out again and again.

When a football team loses a player to injury mid match, they quickly react with

the best replacement player. This team’s agility is possible because they have clear performance metrics on each member of the team. Today that same level of agility is possible for every workforce and every worker with behavioral DATA. It is real; it is proven; and has the potential to transform the global workforce and the economies of all.

For example : •A veteran returning to civilian life had been a sniper in the military. What kind of role does that suggest? Sniper missions are carefully planned, looking at all possible contingencies. Every aspect is documented and reviewed over and over again. It sounds quite similar to project management or sales planning or product development or countless other jobs when the focus is on the individual’s hard-wired strengths rather than the skills of being a sniper.

Virtually every job’s critical factors are found in countless other jobs. When those factors are present, performance is possible just by training the necessary skills and providing some experience and coaching. Conversely, hiring for skills, knowledge or experience does not guarantee successful performance. Understanding their behavioral strengths opens up a world of opportunities for job seekers. Defining jobs with those behavioral strengths allows recruiters to recognize the talent they need in many more candidates.
