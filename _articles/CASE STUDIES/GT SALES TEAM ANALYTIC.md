A New Way to Understand Sales Performance

The Conceptual Foundation

Traditionally, sales performance was based on a two part paradigm of Attitude and Skills & Experience (see Illustration A). If a salesperson had the skills and experience to do the job, but they did not meet performance expectations, it was attributed to an attitude problem. Consequently, management looked to motivational solutions to solve the prob- lem. They promised bonuses if production went up, or threatened punitive measures if it did not. Sometimes these measures were successful; sometimes they were not.

In the 90’s, research using new assessment technology revealed a much more accurate paradigm (see Illustration B). When cognitive abilities and interpersonal competencies (personality traits) were added to the model, it opened the door to a whole new way of understanding and affecting sales performance. If a salesperson does not have the cognitive abilities and personality traits needed for a particular sales role, then even with a great attitude and the necessary skills, they are unlikely to be successful. Similarly, a 4 foot player can be thrilled and enthusiastic about slam dunking a basketball. He can be an excellent shooter, a superb dribbler, and marvelously knowledgeable about bas- ketball. Yet, even with the best coaching and extensive training, he will not be able to slam dunk a basketball without using a ladder. The failure is not because of attitude or skills or lack of hard work; it is because the rim is too high and the other players are too tall.

The Practical Application

Optimizing the performance of any sales team depends upon how well the job capabili- ties of the team match up with the sales strategies and sales processes of a particular company. These job capabilities are only fully understood when the underlying cognitive abilities and behavioral traits of the sales team are measured and translated into terms of those job capabilities.

The following case study illustrates how the Sales Team MRI™ information drives tacti- cal and strategic decisions that can dramatically impact longstanding performance is- sues. Global Technics is a real company, a division of a Fortune 500 corporation, but the name has been changed. The example is dramatic, but not uncommon, except in the magnitude of the problem.

Global Technics had historically been highly profitable, occupying a dominant position in its industry. Competition from a variety of smaller vendors had intensified over the years however, and profit margins had shrunk. In response to this, GT’s management team had decided to change the game in favor of its own greater size and range of services.

A team of 120 salespeople was assembled from the various sales people within the or- ganization’s various service segments. They were charged with going to market with a solutions-oriented value proposition that integrated the various service elements of GT into a comprehensive proposal for the customers. At its announcement, this change in strategy was met with positive excitement and enthusiasm by both customers and the sales team. What was not apparent at that time was the extent to which the new strate- gy challenged the job capabilities of the sales team.

GLOBAL TECHNICS’ CHALLENGES

Traditionally, Global Technics served customers through a simple distributor network, serviced by GT engineers, who specified parts when customers called with a problem.

Now, they must compete for business by providing broader, value-based solutions to wide ranges of customer issues. This is usually a proactive approach rather than a reac- tion to a problem. Salespeople must discover opportunities through a thorough knowl- edge of the customer’s strategic goals and tactical issues. They must then assemble an integrated solution, by collaborating with the various technology resources within the Global Technic corporation.

Summary

The information provided by the Sales Team MRI™ enabled Global Tech to

understand why talented and hardworking people had been unable to real-

ize the full marketplace potential of their unique product and service capa-

bilities. While there was not a quick solution at hand, there was now a clear

course of action, both strategically and tactically. Through a combination of

changes in positions and responsibilities plus a strategic redeployment of

those salespeople who were strong matches into regions with significant

revenue potential, Global Tech was able to salvage several key opportuni-

ties. Approximately one year later, Global Tech was seeing stronger results

and their strategy was gaining traction in multiple areas.

While there is no magic wand for sales success, the Sales Team MRI™

does provide unprecedented information on the capabilities of any group of

sales professionals. It does not presume to tell you if they are good or bad,

but whether your team has the capabilities to successfully drive the sales

strategy and processes of your company or whether those strategies are

viable with your existing sales team. The Sales Team MRI™ gives sales

managers the information they need to immediately determine their team’s

strengths and weaknesses. It allows them to see where they can make

quick tactical adjustments for short term gains, as well as where to make

long term strategic decisions, yielding healthier pipelines and more robust

sales.
