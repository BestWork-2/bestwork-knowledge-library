Picking Call Center Performers – A Case Study

I once wrote a white paper entitled, Traits and Abilities of Call Center

Agents, The Effect on Attrition & Agent Performance. An excellent example

in which those principles were applied within a call center of a Fortune 50

company has come to my attention. It serves to emphasize the importance

of analyzing and understanding the specific set of cognitive abilities and

personality traits that support the performance of each agent. Furthermore,

it is a powerful statement on the impact that such information can have on

profitability, when used effectively.

Several hundred agents completed a 25-minute online questionnaire,

featuring state-of-the-art measurement of cognitive ability and six

personality traits. The resulting DATA was analyzed in correlation with

performance data on the agents. Critical traits and abilities were identified,

which were fundamental for the successful performance of that specific call

center position. This finding essentially meant that an agent lacking those

critical traits would be unlikely to deliver an acceptable level of results.

The reports using the DATA were configured to identify job candidates that

did not have those critical traits and to flag them as a CAUTION at the

appropriate stage in the hiring process. However, change does not always

come easily. Many of the recruiters had relied on interviewing and skills

testing for years, and consequently, they were hesitant to use the DATA

results as a primary element in their decisions. If the results confirmed their

interview-based judgments, all was well. If the results challenged their

interview-based judgments, they tended to disregard the CAUTION flag

and hired the candidate anyway. Following this pattern, 200 CAUTION-

flagged candidates were hired.

In this call center, sixty days was the decision point at which new hires

were evaluated as to continued employment. Of course, much time, energy

and money was invested in each of them over the course of that first sixty

days. Each candidate received intensive training and special attention from

their supervisors and managers. During that period, each candidate also

Of the 200 CAUTION-flagged new hires, only 100 made it past the sixty-

day checkpoint. These candidates had been identified by the DATA as

lacking the critical traits and abilities necessary for successful performance

of the job, yet they were still allowed to consume time, energy and money

needlessly. Many of the others were retained with only marginal

performance. The financial impact of losing these 100 agents at this point

in their tenure was enormous.

What is clear from this example is that interviewing, even when done by

experienced professionals, has difficulty identifying non-performers at an

early stage. Once the non-performers have been screened out,

interviewing plays a much more effective role in understanding how best to

employ the job candidates; how to train them most effectively; and how to

optimize their value to the company. Happily, the recruiters in this example

have now embraced the power of the DATA program as a key tool in using

their own talent and experience to reach higher levels of potential with their

human resources.
