A New Way to Understand Sales Performance

The Conceptual Foundation

Traditionally, sales performance was based on a two part paradigm of Attitude and Skills & Experience (see Illustration A). If a salesperson had the skills and experience to do the job, but they did not meet performance expectations, it was attributed to an attitude problem. Consequently, management looked to motivational solutions to solve the prob- lem. They promised bonuses if production went up, or threatened punitive measures if it did not. Sometimes these measures were successful; sometimes they were not.

In the 90’s, research using new assessment technology revealed a much more accurate paradigm (see Illustration B). When cognitive abilities and interpersonal competencies (personality traits) were added to the model, it opened the door to a whole new way of understanding and affecting sales performance. If a salesperson does not have the cognitive abilities and personality traits needed for a particular sales role, then even with a great attitude and the necessary skills, they are unlikely to be successful. Similarly, a midget can be thrilled and enthusiastic about slam dunking a basketball. He can be an excellent shooter, a superb dribbler, and marvelously knowledgeable about basketball.

Yet, even with the best coaching and extensive training, he will not be able to slam dunk a basketball without using a ladder. The failure is not because of attitude or skills or lack of hard work; it is because the rim is too high and the other players are too tall.

The Practical Application

Optimizing the performance of any sales team depends upon how well the job capabili- ties of the team match up with the sales strategies and sales processes of a particular company. These job capabilities are only fully understood when the underlying cognitive abilities and behavioral traits of the sales team are measured and translated into terms of those job capabilities.

The following case study illustrates how the Sales Team MRI™ information drives tacti- cal and strategic decisions that can dramatically impact longstanding performance is- sues. SmallCo is a real company, but the name has been changed. The example is powerful in illustrating how vital it is for a small company to have the right people in the right jobs. Large companies can generally survive a certain amount of miscasting, be- cause it is often a small percentage of their total workforce. Small companies usually depend on every single employee. When even one is miscast, the effect on the com- pany’s profitability is magnified. With too many employees miscast, the company’s very survival can be at risk.

SMALLCO CASE STUDY

IMPORTANT NOTE: The relevance and immediate value of the MRI information is

directly related to your own knowledge of the current business situation and the

performance issues that are most challenging to the organization. In this case

study of SmallCo, we have set the stage for you, but we can never achieve the in- timacy that you have with your own business issues. Right now imagine yourself as the SmallCo CEO who for 3 years has looked for answers to why his talented team has not achieved the goals that seem so promising.

SmallCo had developed and patented several significant innovations in their industry.

These innovations clearly had the potential to give SmallCo a dominant position. De- spite this promise, for the first few years, the company struggled to produce sales. On those rare occasions when SmallCo was successful, the customers loved the product and the customer service.

SmallCo provides a sophisticated technology to deliver customized training solutions through a customer’s existing IT structure. The sales strategy was to offer an integrated solution that was closely tied to the customer’s own goals. SmallCo felt that this com- prehensive approach gave them a competitive advantage over existing training compa- nies. What was not apparent at that time was the extent to which this strategy chal- lenged the job capabilities of the sales team.

Since each system is configured uniquely for each customer, the integration issues must be understood completely before any meaningful value proposition can be devel- oped. The solutions touch many parts of the customer’s enterprise, and consequently, the sale often becomes quite complex. The decision-making process for SmallCo’s solu- tions typically involves the CEO, CIO, CFO and multiple other stakeholders.

SMALLCO’S TRACK RECORD

Sales teams have been changed 3 times using different hiring strategies

each time.

1.First, industry experts were hired.

2.Then, successful salespeople from large enterprise software compa-

nies were hired.

3.Then, personal referrals from the Board and the executive team were

hired.

Only one salesperson has been successful without extraordinary assis-

tance from the CEO or VP of Sales.

❋ ● ❋

Top professional sales trainers were engaged, specializing in complex

sales. They spend months working with the salespeople and sales man-

agement.

Still…sales goals have not been met for almost 3 years.

❋ ● ❋

Additionally:

- Sales cycles had grown progressively longer.

- There was no consistent sales tracking in place. Each deal’s progress

was evaluated on whatever criteria seemed appropriate to that situation.

- Sales processes were questioned and modified several times.

- Compensation plans were redesigned.

All had little effect.

We suggested taking a look at the capabilities of the current sales team.

Summary

The information provided by the Sales Team MRI™ enabled SmallCo to

understand why talented and hardworking people had been unable to real-

ize the full marketplace potential of their unique and outstanding product.

While there was not a quick solution at hand, there was now a clear course

of action, both strategically and tactically. Through a combination of

changes in positions and responsibilities plus a simplified sales strategy,

management was able to dramatically impact their sales team’s effective-

ness. Approximately one year later, SmallCo had their first profitable quar-

ter and future sales look strong.

The Sales Team MRI™ is not a magic formula for sales success. It does

however, provide unprecedented information on the capabilities of any

group of sales professionals. It does not presume to tell you if they are

good or bad, but whether your team has the capabilities to successfully

drive the sales strategy and processes of your company or whether those

strategies are viable with your existing sales team.
