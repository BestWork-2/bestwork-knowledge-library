A New Perspective

The Issues

Call centers play a central role in the business world today. They are a complex population of thousands of people interacting with customers and prospects to accomplish a wide range of tasks including such diverse activities as collecting debts, collecting information, helping highly emotional customers, selling products or services, and many others. Hiring, training, and managing these agents is critical to each of these call centers. Ironically the impact of telecommunications and technology on this industry has only served to highlight the importance of human performance in determining the productivity, and more im- portant, the profitability of any call center operation. The optimization of any call center business model requires solving the dual problems of attrition and job performance among call center agents. A further irony is that rarely has the same level of sophisticated technology as seen in the telecommunications component been brought to bear on the human components within the centers. Yet in a recent four month period, the application of the concepts discussed in this paper within a major call center produced the following results: Aquisition & Attrition •43% increase in the Interview-to-Offer Ratio •12% increase in the Graduation Rate from new hire training •30% - 40% increase in the Early Retention Rate Job Performance •16% improvement in Orders-per-Hour •46% improvement in Sales Penetration •22% improvement in overall score These are dramatic results. They are results that far exceed even the expectations and goals of the call cen- ter that employed the concepts of this paper. To achieve such results, first requires examining the issues of attrition and job performance within a call center through a new paradigm of understanding.

Attrition is so endemic within the call center industry that many companies just accept 50% - 100+% turnover rates as being a normal part of doing business.

A Different Concept

Traditionally, the understanding of call center agent performance and fit with a particular job has been based on a two part paradigm of Attitude (Values, Motivation) and Skills & Experience (see Illustration A). If an agent had the skills and experience to do the job, but they did not meet performance expecta - tions, it was attributed to an attitude or motivation problem. Consequently, management looked to moti- vational solutions to solve the problem. They promised bonuses if performance improved, or threatened punitive measures if it did not. Sometimes these measures were successful for a period of time; some - times they were not.

If an agent had an outstanding attitude and worked hard but still failed to meet performance expecta - tions, it seemed to be a training issue. If that agent could receive special training and acquire some expe- rience, perhaps the performance would improve. Sometimes the training proved to be beneficial; some- times it did not.

In the 90’s, research using new assessment technology revealed a much more accurate paradigm (see Il- lustration B). When cognitive abilities and interpersonal competencies (personality traits) were added to the model, it opened the door to a whole new way of understanding and affecting agent performance, and most importantly in terms of attrition, to a more effective way of understanding job fit. If an agent does not have the cognitive abilities and personality traits needed for a particular role, then even with a great attitude and the necessary skills, they are unlikely to be successful. Similarly, a midget can be thrilled and enthusiastic about slam dunking a basketball. He can be an excellent shooter, a superb drib- bler, and marvelously knowledgeable about basketball. Yet, even with the best coaching and extensive training, he will not be able to slam dunk a basketball without using a ladder. The failure is not because of attitude or skills or lack of hard work; it is because the basket is too high and the other players are too tall.

It is a case of poor job fit. Even a strong desire to play the game will soon fade in the face of the continual frustration and lack of success that the midget will encounter. When people lack the innate competencies needed to succeed in a particular agent role, even the desire for that job and the need for income will soon be overcome by frustration, stress, and the lack of success.

The Causes of Attrition

Assuming satisfactory working conditions and reasonable compensation, the number one cause of attri- tion in call centers is poor job fit: •A candidate is hired to do a job that requires traits or abilities that the candidate does not have.

•An agent who is successful at one job is transitioned into a job that requires traits or abilities that the agent does not have.

•An agent is promoted into a job that requires traits or abilities that the agent does not have.

The poor job fit is a result of: •The agent lacking the necessary traits or abilities for a well designed job.

•The job being designed to require mutually exclusive traits or abilities. Examples of this are: a.Service-oriented jobs that require persuasive sales efforts b.Empathetic agents in high stress situations or high rejection jobs c.Fast learning speed needed to pass training requirement for routine jobs d.Highly structured or scripted positions that require multi-tasking or job flexibility When the job is well designed and the candidate has the traits and abilities to do the job, the next cause of attrition is ineffective management. That is primarily a result of: •The supervisor or manager lacking the traits or abilities needed for that job.

•The supervisor or manager having the necessary traits or abilities but lacking the proper training in supervisory or management skills.

•The supervisor or manager having the necessary traits, abilities, and training but coaching the agents without knowledge of the agents’ individual strengths and needs.

When viewed through the paradigm of fixed traits and abilities, agent attrition sorts into a very under- standable decision tree of logical conclusions. That understanding opens the door to a wealth of practical solutions.

Applying the Concepts to Call Centers

The effect of this new paradigm on call center management is to refocus time, energy, and money in far more effective ways. Certainly maintaining a positive work environment that promotes good attitudes is important. Also, strong training programs that foster a habit of continual learning in a call center is a key contributor to success. However, these initiatives will enjoy little success if the agents lack the critical per- sonality traits and cognitive ability to execute the necessary strategies and processes. Research and expe- rience has proven beyond a doubt that people are essentially hard-wired to perform some jobs well, some job adequately, and some jobs poorly, regardless of training, management, and incentives. The earlier that this information is available in a process, the greater the impact it will have.

The cognitive abilities and personality traits that are measured with this newer assessment technology do not change over time. They are not affected by training, coaching or motivational incentives. Proper train- ing and coaching can enable an agent to become more effective at working with their inherent traits and abilities to accomplish the necessary results, however the foundational elements do not change. There - fore, it becomes imperative to have accurate and specific information in order to manage job fit so as to optimize agent performance.

The newest Generations (5th, 6th and 7th Generations) of assessment technology offer superb tools for this purpose. These newer instruments offer information with an accuracy and reliability that is far be - yond early efforts that relied on Myers-Briggs or DISC-type instruments in the way that current telecom- munications compares with the switchboards of old. In less than 30 minutes, an accurate picture of a can- didate’s strength as a agent and their fit to a particular call center position can be acquired via a simple online experience.

The classic approach to solving this problem has been to create success profiles or templates by assessing top performers within a call center. Potential job candidates were then assessed and compared to these success patterns, in the hopes of replicating the success of the top performers. Unfortunately, while this methodol- ogy has been helpful in many cases, it has rarely fulfilled its promise. There are inevitable difficulties in defining top performance, and the systems that calculate the comparative matches are fraught with prob- lems that allow poor performers to slip through.

A much more effective application of assessment technology is to identify those candidates that do not have the necessary traits and abilities for the particular call center position. All traits and abilities mea - sured by newer assessments are based on a normative bell curve. Traits and abilities are distributed on a scale from one extreme to the other extreme. By identifying those candidates who fall on the undesirable extreme of a certain trait or ability, it is possible to avoid placing them into a position in which they are unlikely to succeed.

Key Traits and Abilities for Call Center Agents

Key traits and abilities are those traits and abilities that are so important for successful performance in a particular agent role that the lack of them virtually precludes success. When these traits and abilities are missing, extreme effort, additional coaching, and extra support are usually necessary just to achieve an adequate level of performance and attrition is virtually certain.

Cognitive Ability

General Reasoning

Traditional thinking suggests that hiring smart agents is always a good idea. The problem is that the trad- tional definition of smart is dangerously flawed. That flaw in understanding has resulted in hiring can- didates who learn quickly to go through intensive training in order to perform a relatively routine job.

This factor, which is easily measured, is critical in determining exactly what type of products or services a person can sell, even if they have the personality traits that are necessary for sales. General or Fluid Rea- soning is a composite measure of cognitive abilities which include speed of learning, problem solving, logical thinking, verbal reasoning and spatial reasoning. Old thinking of cognitive abilities tended to sug- gest that people were smart or not smart. This is entirely wrong. In the same way as the value of personal- ity traits are only meaningful when compared to those required for a specific job in a specific company under specific circumstances. Simple processes are ideally delivered by agents with slower General Rea- soning. Once they have mastered the process and the knowledge needed for the job, they are engaged by executing that process over and over again. Agents who are faster at General Reasoning tend to become bored by the repetition of a routine process, and ultimately perform poorly compared to those agents who are slower in General Reasoning.

Call strategies that are built around more complex processes require a faster degree of General Reasoning.

Sometimes knowledge must be acquired during the call at a much faster speed. At the same time, that knowledge must be integrated into the available solution options to suggest enough possible benefits to the customer during the call. Therefore, faster processing speed is a clear advantage in a complex process call.

Another disadvantage of faster processing speed is that these agents often communicate at a speed faster than their customers. They also tend to jump ahead in their thinking, instinctively believing that others are following their train of reasoning.

There is an interesting caution concerning the speed of learning of new agents in new hire training. Many new hire training programs are so intensive with so much knowledge compressed into a short time frame that only agents with high learning speed can fully benefit. The problem arises when the actual job is fair- ly routine. The best performers from the training class leave after a relatively short time because the job is not challenging them enough. Ironically, the agents who would most likely succeed at the actual job are the ones who did poorly in the fast-paced training. In fact, they are often screened out during that train- ing experience. By lengthening the training program and adding more repetition and hands-on elements, many companies could positively impact their turnover problems. The additional benefit is that agents who do learn more slowly are very reluctant to leave once they have learned a job well.

Personality Traits

Assertiveness

The essence of any call center sales role is to move a prospective customer to take action. That action may be to make a buying decision, to schedule an appointment, to provide information, or to do some other action that moves the sales process forward. The single most important personality trait for this is As - sertiveness. This is the ability to control a situation. It is the ability to deal with confrontation. It is the ability to be direct with statements or questions. When an agent is highly assertive, they ask the hard questions that either move the sale to a close or that reveal that there is no sale.

Other than yourself, is there anyone else involved in this buying decision?

Is Tuesday or Wednesday better for you?

Of course you want the extended warranty on that, right?

I would recommend that you purchase the upgrade. It is an excellent value.

Do you want that shipped overnight or regular delivery?

They also handle the stalls and objections of their prospects. When the sales process becomes more com- plicated, they tend to stay in control of the next step. They want to make the next call; they do not want to wait for something to happen.

When an agent is low on Assertiveness, they want to avoid confrontation. For them confrontation is the first No or I’m really not interested that they hear from a customer. They tend to be indirect with their ques- tions and statements. They tend to talk about the features and benefits of the product or service, but they never move the prospect to make a decision. Instead, they wait for them to buy.

Extroversion

When one thinks of communicating enthusiasm over the telephone, the image of the smiling, enthusiastic talker generally comes to mind. The personality trait that is exemplified by this image is that of Extrover- sion. Extroverts enjoy people. They enjoy meeting people, talking to people, and interacting with people.

They are usually outgoing in their manner, speaking to almost anyone. They enjoy talking far more than listening. In fact, their listening often consists of listening for an opportunity to start talking. While this fits the stereotype of the TV sitcom salesperson, it is not always the most desirable characteristic for many call center positions. It does however get many candidates hired through a traditional interview process.

Call center processes which depend upon enthusiastic agents that excite the emotions of customers defi- nitely need high extroverts to make those calls. The same calls made by more introverted agents are re- ceived with much less excitement. The same facts are in the presentation but the level of enthusiasm that is communicated is much less. This becomes very important in telephone sales. Since the visual element of communication is missing, it becomes much more important to communicate the necessary level of enthusiasm verbally. Extroverts are far better at doing that.

When calls consist primarily of facts, figures, and specifications, the enthusiastic delivery of an extrovert can be a distraction. The more neutral delivery of a somewhat introverted agent becomes ideal. Similarly, when listening is a critical part of the call process, the agent in the middle of the scale, between highly introverted and highly extroverted, becomes ideal, offering a balance of listening and talking.

Consistency

Consistency is a personality trait that describes how agents deal with change. On one extreme are those agents who are best at following a proven process consistently. They are most comfortable when the call pitch or presentation is scripted for them. Once they have been trained and have some experience, they deliver the same presentation time after time. For certain activity-based telemarketing sales models, this is ideal. These agents perform best selling a standard product or service with a limited number of options.

The other extreme of this trait is flexibility. Flexible agents prefer to adapt the process and the script to each situation. This can be a strength when handling a variety of calls that must meet the needs of each customer. Typically however, the most effective agents tend to be from the middle of the scale. They are flexible, but only when the situation really demands it.

Organization

The middle of the scale on this trait is best for almost all types of call center positions. On one extreme are the extremely organized. They want to plan out everything and work with a schedule of planned tasks. In telemarketing, inbound calls can be upsetting to highly organized agents, who view each unscheduled call as an interruption. In a very short time, the accumulated stress of the interruptions drives the agent to seek another employer.

The other extreme of the scale represents those agents who are purely reactive, responding to each event as it occurs. While this lack of planning makes them ideal for handling inbound calls, their loose follow up can be frustrating for everyone. In these situations, it is important to support the collection of detailed information or any follow up actions with a field-driven software system.

Sensitivity

This is a trait that measures emotional sensitivity and empathy. In call center roles that involve handling calls from customers who are dealing with personal tragedies or distressing situations, such as death, ill- nesses, or being a victim of a crime, an empathetic agent can be critical to provide service in a caring manner. This trait also measures how an agent deals with stress. Highly sensitive agents are rarely suc- cessful in sales positions. The inevitable truth about selling is that there are always more No’s than Yes’s.

Highly sensitive people tend to take this type of rejection personally, and therefore they easily become discouraged, leading to turnover. Agents who are in the middle of the scale tend to handle sales rejection more objectively, and those on the other extreme generally do not notice it.

In telemarketing, salespeople who are less sensitive are usually more comfortable with the sedentary na- ture of call centers. When agents are more sensitive, they require more activity. By recognizing this, more frequent breaks can often satisfy this need.

Team Motivation

Team motivation describes the competitive environment that is most motivational for an agent. Individu- ally competitive agents thrive on individual competition. They need recognition for outstanding perfor- mance, and they want to be seen as the best by their peers. Lacking this recognition, they can become dis- couraged and demotivated despite their actual success. Team competitive agents thrive on group or team- based competition. Case Studies of Call Center Positions

Business-to-Business Sales

A business premiums sales company provided high quality customized pens, calculators, calendars, and other items to companies all over the world. In addition to calling existing customers, new prospects were generated by Web traffic and targeted mailing with sample products. Key factors for successful agents were: Agents with fast cognitive processing (General Reasoning) were initially prized for their training perfor- mance and often for their actual job performance. Unfortunately, these fast learners quickly became bored with the routine of the job and became attrition statistics. Slower learners needed more time to acquire the knowledge that was necessary to drive the sale, but enhanced catalog systems minimized that issue.

Highly assertive agents were able to qualify the customers marketing challenges and drive them to deci- sions between a few targeted options. Less assertive agents tended to rely on the customers to make their own decisions.

A large insurance company created a special call center to launch a new sales strategy. The agents were drawn from the existing call center, and each was a top performer in that environment. In the original role, agents received inbound calls from policy holders asking questions about their policies. The agents looked up the information and relayed it to the customers. The company saw this as an opportunity to tell the customers about additional financial services or ways to enhance their current coverage.

Original Role - Inbound Customer Questions

In the original role, low or mid General Reasoning was ideal. The agent simply looked up the customer’s policy information and answered their questions. The job was repetitive, and once learned, presented no real challenges. Low and mid Assertiveness was perfect. High or mid Consistency matched the pure process nature of the job. High or mid Team Motivation ensured a strong service attitude.

New Role - Inbound Customer Question Transition to Sales Information

By recruiting the top performers from the original role to fill the new role, the company was following the traditional course taken by most businesses. Without an understanding of the hard-wired nature of traits and abilities, as measured with new assessment technology, it makes sense to use proven performers for a new role. Unfortunately, even winning the Kentucky Derby does not qualify one for an All Star perfor- mance in the NBA. Jobs can be changed much more easily than people.

Summary

These case studies and many others are convincing evidence that personality traits and abilities are foundational in determining who will be successful in any call center role. While the critical traits may vary in different agent positions, each type of call center role depends upon critical traits, without which success is unlikely. Today, 6th and 7th Generation assessment technology can quickly and accurately mea1 - sure these traits and abilities in less than 30 minutes. Reports are written specifically to describe job be- haviors in a way that requires no training or expert consulting to understand. This technology drives re- sults such as those noted at the beginning of this paper.

Reducing Attrition Through Hiring & Selection of Agents

The most powerful impact in reducing attrition is made by identifying those candidates who are poor fits for the job. This gives the call center management several positive options. By hiring only from a pool of candidates who are either good or adequate fits with the job, turnover will be reduced and performance will increase. Even when circumstances require the hiring of some candidates to fill seats, who are poor fits, the knowledge of what the fit problems are enables management to make better deployment deci - sions. Poor fits are placed in roles that offer the least exposure or risk from unsatisfactory performance. Enhancing Performance Through Competency Management

With performance improvement as the goal of every call center manager or senior executive, it only makes sense to understand the capabilities of the agents as the first step in any coaching effort and well before spending any money on training. Once that information is in hand, the likely success of any strate- gic decisions involving the agents is evident. With that same information, tactical decisions, such as de- ployment or support requirements also become clear. Training can now be focused so as to be most effec- tive, with the appropriate training dollars being spent on the right people. Those agents who are miscast for their roles can then be redirected into other assignments that are more suited to their strengths. When agents are cast into the right roles, they become productive much quicker and of course, usually remain in their positions longer. Since the managers start off with specific coaching information for each agent, they are able to influence new agents much earlier in the cycle.

In the same way, existing teams of agents can be analyzed through competency inventories. This allows the call center management to coordinate their performance strategies and job requirements with the available capabilities within the team. As better information is provided, more effective decisions can be made.

At this point in time, the newest assessment technology represents a competitive advantage for call cen- ters. Within a year or two, the use of such information will be necessary just to remain competitive. In three or more years, adopting these assessment applications will be an attempt to survive.
