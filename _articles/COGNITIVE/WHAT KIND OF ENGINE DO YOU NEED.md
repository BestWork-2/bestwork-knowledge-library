What Kind of Engine Do You Need in That Car?

My guess is that when you are buying a car, one of your key questions is

“What kind of engine do you need in that car?”. Does the car need to be

fast and responsive? Does it need to handle heavy loads? …in the snow?

…up hills? What about fuel consumption? Maintenance? It is important to

match the engine with exactly what kind of performance you expect from

the car. When hiring a person for a particular job, it is equally important to

match the kind of performance you need with that person’s engine. With

people, the engine is their cognitive abilities or how they learn and process

information.

Human reasoning is a collection of separate abilities, each dealing with a

different type of information. The most relevant ones for the business world

are numerical reasoning, verbal reasoning, spatial reasoning, logical

thinking and problem solving. There are people who learn very quickly and

seem to solve problems easily. On the other hand, they also get bored

easily, and need constant challenges to stay engaged with their job.

Conversely, there are people who are geniuses at routine, happily dealing

with fixed processes that seldom change. Some people have a strategic

focus, always looking ahead, while others are more tactical, focusing on the

short term. Others deal with the immediate operations, not being troubled

by future concerns.

When these different cognitive abilities are unknown, talented people are

often placed into roles that are poor fits. They may be asked for a speed of

learning and agility with change that they don’t have. Others may breeze

through a challenging training program to find themselves in simple

repetitive jobs with little challenge at all. When this happens, poor

performance or turnover is the outcome. At best, the talent within the

enterprise is not optimized.

## Page 2

One of the most pervasive and misleading concepts about intelligence is

smart. The danger in the word is that is doesn’t really tell what smart

means in terms of job behavior. Sometimes it describes someone whose

knowledge about their area of responsibility is extensive, having been

acquired through years of experience. Sometimes it describes someone

who is a quick study, picking up an understanding of almost any situation in

only a short period of time. These are dramatically different types of smart.

Pitchers and catchers may both be talented baseball players, but they are

definitely not interchangeable in their positions.

Today, science has made it possible to see what each person’s speci fic

cognitive abilities are in terms of job behaviors. This enables the job seeker

to recognize the most promising types of jobs for them. The employer can

also see how well the job candidates match specific jobs.
