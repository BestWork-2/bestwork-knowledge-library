Teamwork is many things to many businesses. It is assumed to be a necessary compo- nent of top organizations. It is a convenient goal for training initiatives. It is a quality that virtually everyone salutes. It is a compelling theme for motivational speeches. It is the Grail for dedicated HR professionals. The methodologies used to pursue this Grail are myriad. All achieve some degree of success, yet all fall somewhat short of what is expected. This paper is an examination of some of those methods in the light of new psychometrics that reveal a much deeper understanding of the underlying structure of teams.

Level I Analysis of Team Topology

There are multiple variables that affect the performance of work teams. The most critical of these variables are only measurable using the latest psychometric instruments. For 1 over twenty years, in thousands of cases, experience has proven that two things are foundational in understanding how teams function:

- The Cognitive Abilities of the individuals on the team - These refer to general or

fluid reasoning, which includes speed of processing information, logical think-

ing, problem solving, and speed of learning. By themselves, these are interesting but when you map each member’s score into a composite model of the team, a world of information is revealed. The MRI Concept or How To Engineer High Performance Teams

Several decades ago, a person experiencing severe headaches might have consulted the brightest and best medical minds of that time, attempting to discover the cause of the pain. The best medical thinking of that time might have recommended exploratory brain surgery as the most promising option. Today, that same person with the same headaches would be directed to have an MRI (Magnetic Resonance Image) taken. In less than an hour, the physician would have a 3-dimensional blueprint of the patient’s brain, revealing hitherto unseen information on the functional elements that might cause the headaches. Instead of surgery, the physician might prescribe taking some green pills for 10 days. The diagnosis that makes safer and more effective treatments possible is based on a higher level of information. Today’s doctor is not necessarily smarter than the one who prescribed surgery decades ago; he is simply working with better and more specif- ic information.

The traditional methodologies of building more effective teams are based on very rudi- mentary models. If assessments are used, they are typically DISC-type instruments or Myers-Briggs models. While this represents the best thinking of the first half of the last century, it has little in common with the power of newer generations of psychometric instruments and current psychological theory.

Today, it is possible to accurately measure an individual’s cognitive abilities:

- How quickly they learn

- How they solve problems

- Whether they focus best on immediate issues, tactical issues or strategic issues

The managing partner assembled three or four person teams composed of the appropriate specialists. They would work closely together to provide the services needed by the client. Cognitive abilities or speed of rea- soning share a similar depth of field characteristic. Slower reasoning speeds enable indi- viduals to concentrate on the immediate situation. Very fast reasoning speeds enable individuals to focus their attention on strategic issues, projecting future possibilities that might occur based on alternative actions. Individuals whose processing speeds are in the middle of the bell curve focus best on tactical issues.

In business, tactical issues include profits margins, quality standards, customer service, and product delivery. Strategic issues include potential threats in the marketplace, tech- nological changes in the industry, potential opportunities that might derive from those changes, the dynamics of the economy, and the direction of research and development.

Clearly, the topology of this executive team is set for tactical excellence. Indeed, that is exactly what they had achieved: strong margins, high quality, excellent customer ser- vice, and on-time delivery. What was missing from the executive team’s competencies was a focus on strategic or long term issues. It is important to understand that every - thing is relative to something. The team did strategic planning. They considered strate- gic issues. The problem was that no one brought binoculars to the ball game. They could only see so far. What they considered strategic was really tactical compared to other businesses. In effect, competitors with more strategic thinkers out-innovated them and out-positioned them in the market. They were being leapfrogged in their own pond.

The second row in TABLE C shows the team’s preference for either innovative thinking or conventional thinking. Simplicity of Action

How hard is it to do this? When medical MRI’s became readily available, it made no sense to perform surgery without first reviewing the MRI information. In the same way, it makes little sense to invest thousands of dollars of time, energy and money in training programs and team initiatives without first knowing the topology of the team. The as- sessment of team members requires less than 30 minutes online. In less than 24 hours, the collective map of the team is ready for review by executives, HR professionals, trainers, or consultants. In fifteen minutes or less, the average group is using the infor- mation and integrating it into their own processes with minimal training or explanation.

While expert assistance is always available, the presentation of the team topology in - formation is designed to minimize the need for outside help. The transition is as simple as going from a typewriter to a computer keyboard and word processing. Almost every- thing is the same, but with some slightly different terms and functions. It is the differ- ence in potential that is dramatic.

Without a doubt, the topology of teams can shatter every paradigm currently held re- garding training, management and hiring.
