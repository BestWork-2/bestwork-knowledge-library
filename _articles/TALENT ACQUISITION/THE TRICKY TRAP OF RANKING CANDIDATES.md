The Tricky Trap of Ranking Candidates

Traditional hiring methodologies predating the use of behavioral DATA

generally depended upon some way of ranking the job candidates. The

most common method was to test the “top performers” and create a

profile using their scores. Job candidates could then be tested and their

scores compared to the scores of the “top performers.” The degree to

which they matched was expressed as a percentage. 70% was usually

considered to be acceptable. 80% was terrific and if they were 90%, hire

them before they could leave the building. A more rudimentary method

simply sorted them into Good, Okay and Not Acceptable or Green, Yellow

and Red. While this seemed to make sense and it was easy to let the

percentages or colors make decisions, there were serious shortcomings.

The basic problem is that all data is not equally important. Combining the

various data points dilutes the key facts with the other elements that are

less critical to the performance of a particular job. For example, an

excellent assessment which produced good data, profiled a sales team

creating a template to compare with job candidates. One of the

candidates was a 92% match to the template, indicating an outstanding

hire. However, the missing 8% was the inability to persuade or sell

anything.

This can be demonstrated using a baseball team. The players can be

easily ranked in order of hitting effectiveness using the batting average

that each player has. The pitchers can be ranked using their ERA. There

are a world of metrics available to rank the players in any singe category.

But can we rank them as baseball players? How does the pitchers’ ERA

compare to the slugging percentage of the outfielders? How do stolen

bases compare to fielding percentage?

In business, sales teams have an array of metrics to choose from.

Accurate stack ranking even a sales team becomes questionable. Is it total

revenue? Profit margins? Percentage of new business? The ranking must

be based on a single metric to have any accuracy.

Stack ranking job candidates is not possible in any credible way without a

common metric. It is possible to create a metric from a series of subjective

decisions if they are structured in a simple and logical format that is used

consistently. The advantage that BestWork provides is that all candidates

that pass through the Critical Factor Screening can do the job. Therefore

the ranking of that group has minimal risk. As data is collected, the

process and the metrics can become more useful.
