## Page 1

Simple Hiring Facts

My son’s girlfriend is smart, beautiful, funny, creative and they are

wonderfully in love. She also has celiac disease, which means that she

must only eat foods that are gluten-free. It does not matter that the

restaurant has three Michelin stars. It does not matter that the chickens are

free range with no trace of antibiotics or helpful hormones or that they

listened to classical music continually while being raised. It only matters

that the dish she is eating is gluten-free. There is a powerful truth in this

simple fact, and that truth can totally change the world of hiring.

For several decades, psychologists have known that people have hard-

wired personality traits that basically remain the same throughout their

lives. These traits are not affected by training, coaching or motivational

incentives. While this has long been established, for many years, the ability

to measure these traits and to understand how they related to job

performance required special training and sophisticated psychometric

instruments. The only way businesses even glimpsed the information was

through crude profiling methods, trying to compare top performers to job

candidates. Instead of revealing the potential of the data that measured the

personality traits, profiling worked to blur the specific meaning of the data

behind a simple percentage match to the profile of the “top performers.”

Unfortunately, it was like having a percentage match to how a well a

restaurant was suited for my son’s girlfriend. Factoring the quality of the

food, the ambiance, the service, the wine list and other things, along with

their gluten-free options might produce a solid 92% match, but in reality, the

only fact that mattered was whether or not the specific dish she wanted to

order was itself gluten-free.

Hiring good performers is remarkably the same. If a company is hiring

salespeople to persuade customers to purchase their products or services

rather than those of their competitors, the key or critical factor is whether or

not the person can persuade other people to do that. They may be friendly,

well-dressed, well educated and have excellent organizational skills, but if

they cannot persuade people to make a buying decision, the other assets

do not matter. All of the fine qualities in the world cannot offset the absence

of the critical behavior necessary for that job.

Good news! Now it is possible to easily see if a person has the personality

traits necessary to deliver the job behaviors that are critical to any type of

job. It does not require experts or any special training. The latest

generation of reports are written in plain language, describing how a job

candidate or an existing employ can or cannot do each part of the job in

question. It even explains any particular challenges that they may face in

that job.

## Page 3

In addition to understanding job behaviors, it is also important to know how

a person thinks and learns. Cognitive abilities are also easily measured

with the newest tools such as BestWork. Applying these simple hiring facts

to a selection process with the same diligence as ensuring the dinner

choice is gluten free can virtually make bad hires optional.

NOTE FROM THE AUTHOR: For the purpose of making the point, I state these facts in absolute terms.

There are inevitable exceptions to everything, but in my experience,

successful companies do not hope for exceptions in their business plans.

“Basketball stars are tall.” is an absolute statement that does not deny the

odd player like Spud Webb or Mugsy Boles. It does recognize that they

were exceptions. However, no NBA team had its scouts out looking for

short players. Spud and Mugsy had to have extraordinary skills and

determination just to make up for what they lacked in height, and the

novelty of their accomplishment was exciting to see. Unfortunately, when

the job does not fit the strengths of an individual, they too must put forth

extraordinary effort just be adequate in that job. It is almost impossible to

achieve excellence.
