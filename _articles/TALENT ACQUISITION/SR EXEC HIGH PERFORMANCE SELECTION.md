Senior Executive High Performance Selection Process

1.BestWork Quick Screen Chart

The BestWork Quick Screen Chart only looks at the Critical Job Behaviors

that are necessary for anyone to be successful in this particular job. The

purpose is to screen out any candidates that cannot do the job.

This sales candidate cannot close sales and cannot control the sales

process. Therefore, they must be screened out. Quick Screens can be

used for all types of jobs. Examples of other Critical Factors are: •For management roles - Directing the actions of others •For financial roles - Working accurately with details •For call centers - Handling inbound calls •For manufacturing - Consistently complying with safety rules •For retail sales - Greeting and approaching customers 2.BestWork Job Report The BestWork Job Report describes how each candidate can deliver the job behaviors of this particular job. Some job behaviors may be quite easy, while others may present challenges. Comparing the Job Reports of the candidates enables you to select the ones that seem to present the most favorable combination of strengths and abilities for the role in question.

3.Resumés & References - Evaluate Knowledge & Experience

At this point, it is time to review the resumés, references and other

information on the candidates you have selected. There are few poor

resumés and many that are well written. The purpose of this review is to

eliminate any candidates that lack necessary skills, certifications or

experience that cannot be acquired after hiring. A candidate with the right

strengths and abilities can often become an excellent performer when

given the proper training and direction, even if they lack knowledge and

experience in the beginning. Requiring extensive experience is often itself

a mistake when that experience is not adaptable to the new enterprise.

Requiring certain degrees, even advanced degrees, generally has little to

do with the candidate’s ability to perform the job.

4.Behavioral Event-Based Interview with BestWork Questions

BestWork provides behavioral event-based interview questions that are

directed at the specific challenges each candidate will have in the job,

based on their strengths and abilities. The important thing at this point is

to eliminate any candidates that do not seem acceptable after the

interview. Too often, interviews become an inclusionary process with the

interviewers listening for reasons to include them in the final selection

process. That will come later. The BestWork questions will help to focus on

actual behaviors, not simply the enthusiasm or energy of the candidates.

5.Personal Interview (second interview)

This is where the first true selection takes place. All unacceptable or

questionable candidates have been excluded at this point. Clients add

interview questions related to the company’s culture or other special

elements. The objective is to find our why the candidate chose this

company, and to learn more about their personal history and goals.

6.Match with Boss

Once it has been determined that the candidate can do the job, the next

question is Can they do it here? The first step in answering that is to look

at how they match up with their boss, the person who will be directing

their work and evaluating their performance.

The example below shows 3 COO candidates, A, B and C plus the CEO,

shown as an asterisk. On the first row, the CEO is shown to be highly

persuasive and challenging, therefore needing a COO who can handle

that. Candidate A is a good match being similarly persuasive and

challenging. Candidates B and C are not, and faced with such a strong

CEO, they would become essentially silent yes’s. This is further

emphasized by the second row, which shows the CEO as a seeker of

change and new ideas. They need a COO who can challenge this when

necessary and work with the CEO to choose the right changes.

7.Fit with Team

Executive teams require a variety of strengths and abilities, just as they

require a variety of skills and knowledge. Inevitably there will be gaps and

differences between various members of the team. The question for the

selection process is to understand the value that the candidate brings to

the team and whether or not their value can be realized in view of those

differences. Certainly, BestWork is the primary tool in recognizing the

differences and engineering how to work with them.

The example below is of a senior executive team of five considering a

candidate for a vice president role on the team. The candidate is shown

with the red circles and the distribution of the five executives are shown

with the encircled numbers.

The first row shows that the candidate processes information more slowly

than the rest of the team. While this is not necessarily a disqualification, it

is an important factor. The second line shows that the candidate has a low

need for details while the other executives have a high need for details.

This would make for a difficult working relationship with dramatically

different expectations in terms of information. The third row indicates that

the candidate favors the exception rather than following rules and

procedures. The other members of the team are much more consistent

and dependable with the rules and procedures. The candidate is an outlier

in each of the three areas. One might be workable, but the combination of

two or three presents an unacceptable obstacle to efficient collaboration

and communication.

8.Executive Footprint Review

If this is a senior executive position or one that is critical for the success of the enterprise, the selection process must consider the entire executive footprint of that position. This includes not only the boss and the executive team, but also the support team and the immediate subordinates of the that position. Are the strengths complimentary? Are there significant conflicts? Is additional support necessary? What potential weaknesses are there? Overall, is it a solid team?

9.30 Day Projections

This is the place where the executive team results are shared with the final

candidates. For the highest level positions, the executive footprint results

can be included. At this point, the candidate must demonstrate an

understanding of the enterprise and how they can play their role in its

operation. The candidate must look beyond the hiring decision and

project what they expect to do in the first 30 days. There is certainly

information they do not have, and their vision may not be exactly the same

as the current leadership. The key is how well they expressed it and how

well they understand what they bring to the role.

10.Your Feelings

Far too often, hiring decisions are made first with “gut” feelings with

information added later in the process. With the easy availability of

behavioral DATA that is directly related to job performance, putting

information first is essential to high performance selection. There is always

a place for feelings. Once you have identified the candidate you want to
