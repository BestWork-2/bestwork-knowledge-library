Types of Hiring Information

There are countless versions of hiring processes and selection systems. Overall,

the success rate of hiring a job candidate who actually performs the job as

expected is 43% according to the Society for Human Resource Management.

That includes systems using traditional assessment tools, behavioral event-

based interviews and a variety of clever techniques. The good news is that it all changed with the advent of BestWork, a completely new generation of psychometric instruments designed to integrate directly into business processes. Rather than try to hire some version of an ideal candidate, BestWork first eliminates all of the bad hires from the candidate pool. Everyone left can do the job adequately. Now the goal is to select from those the ones who can best do the job. The information to do that comes not only from BestWork, but from several other sources. The effectiveness of that process depends upon knowing the quality of each type of information. Some is accurate and reliable. Some is subjective and must be accepted with that caveat. Necessary Elements

Some jobs have definite elements that present a binary screening point.

Examples : •Do you have a car?

•Are you willing to travel?

•Do you have the necessary certifications or licenses?

•Do you speak Spanish?

•Can you work weekends?

The answers can bet easily and inexpensively acquired online or with a phone

call. The answers are also verifiable. A “No” answer exits that candidate.

Critical Behavioral Factors

These are job behaviors that are absolutely necessary for successful

performance of the job. This is the primary screening point for all jobs as it

presents a clear binary decision. The candidate either has the necessary traits

and abilities or they do not. BestWork provides this information accurately and

reliably. The binary decision enables unacceptable candidates to be screened

out automatically.

Behavioral Performance Factors - Positive

Most jobs have certain job behaviors that support specific parts of the job but

that are not critical. They do however, provide advantages to that part of job

performance. Examples : •High level of interacting with people - This supports sales prospecting and networking.

•High level of communicating energy and enthusiasm - This helps

create engaging sales presentations. It promotes a warm and friendly

atmosphere with customers and within the workplace.

•Ability to react to unplanned events - Call center agents with this trait

can take inbound calls. Similarly, retail associates can handle walk-in

customers.

•Extreme attention to details - This is a tremendous asset in technical

positions that involve working with critical and even dangerous details.

There are some traits that are acceptable for a particular job, but they need

some support to be fully effective. Examples : •Organization & planning - A moderate or even lower level of organization & planning might be acceptable most of the time for some jobs. However, for high priority projects, it may be wise to add some oversight by team members that are more detail-focused. There are also many organizational tools available with technology that can be useful in this area.

•Prefers to work alone/needs little interaction with others - Managers

and supervisors particularly, need to engage each member of their

team frequently and regularly. Tracking this interaction is a easy way to

ensure that it happens.

•Sensitive to stress - It is extremely important for individuals who are

sensitive to stress to work with some form of stress management.

Activity of some kind is helpful.

Skills Testing - Jobs that depend upon specific skills can test candidates for

those jobs to verify their skills. It is important to understand the proven

effectiveness of whatever testing is available. Skills testing is quite different from knowledge testing. The fact that a candidate has the knowledge does not necessarily mean that they can perform the job. It is also possible that a candidate can learn the skills or knowledge in a reasonable time frame.

BestWork’s measurement of learning speed can be important in that

consideration.

Experience

•General Experience - What has the candidate done that demonstrates

their capabilities, ambition, perseverance, leadership, initiative or other

characteristics that suggest they would be a good employee. This is

purely subjective. It may be helpful or not, but it should be explored.

•Specific Experience - Does the candidate have experience performing

the same job? What do they see as being di fferent with a new

employer? Why did they change?

Communication - The interview itself is a demonstration of the candidate’s

communication ability. However, it is important to focus on what level of

communication ability is needed for the job in question, A poor communicator in

an interview could well be a star performer in many jobs. Conversely an

excellent communicator in the interview who wonderfully engages every

interviewer may well be a poor performer in the job.

Video Interview

Video interviews are common in some hiring situations. The glitch is that some

people are uncomfortable on camera, which affects their interview regardless of

how they might perform the job. Others love being in front of a camera, and

consequently they interview well in that situation, regardless of how they may

perform the job.

References

It is generally reassuring to receive a positive reference about a job candidate. It is important to realize that the reference is a subjective opinion about circumstances in a different place. Also, most companies, as a standard practice, limit their references to neutral or somewhat positive statements.

Sales Special - Motivation Match

Sales organizations use different forms of motivation with their sales teams. A

common strategy is fostering individual competition among the sales team with

the salesperson selling the most receiving an award that may include a bonus or

a prize of some kind, This can be affective among the salespeople who are hard-

wired to enjoy that kind of competition. The other common form of motivation is

to incentivize the entire team by offering a reward for everyone if the team

achieves its collective sales goal. It is important to know which kind of

motivation works with each member of the team. BestWork sales reports show

this.
