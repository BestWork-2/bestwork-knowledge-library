What About Those Other Things?

Culture fit…? Dedication…? Passion…? Commitment…?

Hiring decisions are always important. For many companies, they can be

critical. Understanding this, companies have used a myriad of methods,

tools and strategies in an effort to hire the “right person.” Not all of these

are effective. In fact, some elements thought to be helpful, are just as likely

to favor poor hiring decisions. Not all information is the same quality, and

recognizing the differences can be a powerful step toward more effective

hiring decisions.

The most effective information available on the potential performance of a

job candidate is from the objective measurement of their hard-wired

personality traits and their cognitive abilities. This directly affects job

performance, and BestWork can measure these these easily and

inexpensively.

The next most reliable information is found in jobs that require a specific

set of skills & knowledge that can be objectively tested or that can be

demonstrated. Unfortunately, many jobs require soft skills and experience

that cannot be tested.

Most other types of information used in making selection decisions

depend upon subjective interpretation. This includes resumés, interviews

and referrals. Unfortunately, the interpretation varies from person to

person, which tends to confuse the decision. A success rate of 50% is

normal for professional interviewers. Resumés are typically filled with

exaggerations and generalized claims of accomplishments that may not

even be relevant for the job in question. All referrals are based on

subjective evaluations.

Every factor that you add to the selection process that is not

objectively measurable introduces errors into the decisions.

This is a critical truth that undermines countless well-meant hiring

decisions, seeking passionate, dedicated, committed employees who fit

the company’s culture. Without a doubt, these characteristics are

important and desirable. The catch is in knowing how to actually find

them. The solution is to look deeper to the behavior that is the actual

objective. There is always a behavior that is desired, and the subjective

term is being used to describe it. For example: A company may have different behaviors associated with a characteristic, but it is still the behavior that is paramount in making sound hiring decisions. The key question to ask to identify the behavior is “What is the person doing or not doing that you are describing with that term?” Once the behaviors are known, BestWork can easily measure each candidate’s ability to deliver that behavior in the job.
