A New Perspective

The Conceptual Foundation

Traditionally, sales performance was based on a two part paradigm of Attitude and Skills & Experience (see Illustration A). If a salesperson had the skills and experience to do the job, but they did not meet per- formance expectations, it was attributed to an attitude problem. Consequently, management looked to motivational solutions to solve the problem. They promised bonuses if production went up, or threatened punitive measures if it did not. Sometimes these measures were successful; sometimes they were not.

In the 90’s, research using new assessment technology revealed a much more accurate paradigm (see Il- lustration B). When cognitive abilities and interpersonal competencies (personality traits) were added to the model, it opened the door to a whole new way of understanding and affecting sales performance. If a salesperson does not have the cognitive abilities and personality traits needed for a particular sales role, then even with a great attitude and the necessary skills, they are unlikely to be successful. Similarly, a player 4 feet tall can be thrilled and enthusiastic about slam dunking a basketball. He can be an excellent shooter, a superb dribbler, and marvelously knowledgeable about basketball. Yet, even with the best coaching and extensive training, he will not be able to slam dunk a basketball without using a ladder. The failure is not because of attitude or skills or lack of hard work; it is because the rim is too high and the other players are too tall.

Applying the Concepts

The effect of this new paradigm on sales management is to refocus the time, energy, and money in far more effective ways. Certainly maintaining a positive work environment that promotes good attitudes is important. Also, strong training programs that foster a habit of continual learning in a sales team is a key contributor to success. However, these initiatives will enjoy little success if the members of the sales team lack the critical personality traits and cognitive ability to execute the necessary sales strategies and sales processes.

The classic approach to solving this problem has been to create success profiles or templates by assessing top performers within a sales organization. Potential job candidates were then assessed and compared to these success patterns, in the hopes of replicating the success of the top performers. Unfortunately, while this methodology has been helpful in many cases, it has rarely fulfilled its promise. There are inevitable difficulties in defining top performance, and the systems that calculate the comparative matches are fraught with problems that allow poor performers to slip through.

A much more effective application of assessment technology is to identify those candidates that do not have the necessary traits and abilities for the particular sales position. All traits and abilities measured by newer assessments are based on a normative bell curve. Traits and abilities are distributed on a scale from one extreme to the other extreme. By identifying those candidates who fall on the undesirable extreme of a certain trait or ability, it is possible to avoid placing them into a position in which they are unlikely to succeed.

Key Traits and Abilities for Professional Sales Positions

Key traits and abilities are those traits and abilities that are so important for successful performance in a particular sales role that the lack of them virtually precludes success. When these traits and abilities are missing, extreme effort, additional coaching, and extra support are usually necessary just to achieve an adequate level of production.

Cognitive Ability

General Reasoning

While personality traits such as assertiveness and extroversion seem to be obvious areas of interest when hiring winning salespeople, the most overlooked factor in sales success is cognitive ability. This factor, which is easily measured, is critical in determining exactly what type of products or services a person can sell, even if they have the personality traits that are necessary for sales. General or Fluid Reasoning is a composite measure of cognitive abilities which include speed of learning, problem solving, logical think- ing, verbal reasoning and spatial reasoning. Old thinking of cognitive abilities tended to suggest that people were smart or not smart. This is entirely wrong. In the same way as the value of personality traits are only meaningful when compared to those required for a specific job in a specific company under spe- cific circumstances. Simple sales processes that involve basic products or services are ideally sold by salespeople with slower General Reasoning. Once they have mastered a sales approach and a set of prod- uct knowledge, they are engaged by executing that process over and over again. Salespeople who are faster at General Reasoning tend to become bored by the repetition of a routine sales process.

Sales strategies that are built around selling solutions to more complex problems require a faster degree of General Reasoning. faster speed. At the same time, that knowledge must be integrated into the available solution options to suggest enough possible benefits to move the sale forward. Therefore, faster processing speed is a clear advantage in selling complex sales or solution sales.

The disadvantage to faster processing speed is that these salespeople often communicate at a speed faster than their prospects. They also tend to jump ahead in their thinking, instinctively believing that everyone else is following their train of reasoning.

There is an interesting caution concerning the speed of learning of new salespeople in sales training.

Many sales training programs are so intensive with so much knowledge compressed into a short time frame that only salespeople with high learning speed can fully benefit. The problem arises when the actu- al sales job is fairly routine. The best performers from the training class leave after a relatively short time because the sale is not challenging them enough. Ironically, the salespeople who would most likely suc- ceed at the actual sales job are the ones who did poorly in the fast-paced training. In fact, they are often screened out during that training experience. By lengthening the training program and adding more repe- tition and hands-on elements, many companies could positively impact their turnover problems.

Personality Traits

Assertiveness

The essence of any sales role is to move a prospective customer to take action. That action may be to make a buying decision, to schedule an appointment, to provide information, or to do some other action that moves the sales process forward. The single most important personality trait for this is Assertiveness.

This is the ability to control a situation. It is the ability to deal with confrontation. It is the ability to be direct with statements or questions. When a salesperson is highly assertive, they ask the hard questions that either move the sale to a close or that reveal that there is no sale.

Other than yourself, is there anyone else involved in this buying decision?

If I can make those changes in the product, are you prepared to give me the order today?

What is your budget for this project?

When did you plan on actually making this purchase?

Do you want that shipped overnight or regular delivery?

They also handle the stalls and objections of their prospects. When the sales process becomes more com- plicated, they tend to stay in control of the next step. They want to make the next call; they do not want to wait for something to happen.

When a salesperson is low on Assertiveness, they want to avoid confrontation. For them this is the No or I’m really not interested. They tend to be indirect with their questions and statements. They tend to talk about the features and benefits of the product or service, but they never move the prospect to make a de- cision. Instead, they wait for them to buy.

Extroversion

When one thinks of selling, the image of the smiling, enthusiastic talker generally comes to mind. The personality trait that is exemplified by this image is that of Extroversion. Extroverts enjoy people. They enjoy meeting people, talking to people, and interacting with people. They are usually outgoing in their manner, speaking to almost anyone. They enjoy talking far more than listening. In fact, their listening of- ten consists of listening for an opportunity to start talking. While this fits the stereotype of the TV sitcom salesperson, it is not always the most desirable characteristic for many professional sales positions. It does however get many candidates hired through a traditional interview process.

Sales processes which depend upon enthusiastic sales presentations that excite the emotions of prospects definitely need high extroverts to deliver those presentations. The same presentations delivered by more introverted salespeople are received with much less excitement. The same facts are in the presentation but the level of enthusiasm that is communicated is much less. This becomes very important in telephone sales. Since the visual element of communication is missing, it becomes much more important to commu- nicate the necessary level of enthusiasm verbally. Extroverts are far better at doing that.

When sales presentations are primarily facts, figures, and specifications, the enthusiastic delivery of an extrovert can be a distraction. The more neutral delivery of a somewhat introverted salesperson becomes ideal. Similarly, when listening is a critical part of the sales process, the salesperson in the middle of the scale becomes ideal, offering a balance of listening and talking.

Complex sales processes demand different types of presentations at different times and to different audi- ences. Many successful sales organizations have found that teaming salespeople together allows each presentation to be led by the optimum personality.

Consistency

Consistency is a personality trait that describes how salespeople deal with change. On one extreme are those salespeople who are best at following a proven sales path consistently. They are most comfortable when the sales pitch or presentation is scripted for them. experience, they deliver the same presentation time after time. For certain activity-based sales models, this is ideal. They perform best selling a standard product or service with a limited number of options.

The other extreme of this trait is flexibility. Flexible salespeople prefer to adapt the presentation and the sales process to each situation. This can be a strength when selling consultative solutions that are cus - tomized for the needs of each customer. Typically however, the most effective salespeople tend to be from the middle of the scale. They are flexible, but only when the situation really demands it. They are able to build upon what has worked in the past. Extremely flexible salespeople tend to vary the products or ser- vices so much that production and delivery becomes a problem.

Contributing Traits for Professional Sales Positions

Contributing traits are those traits that facilitate sales production or hinder it, but they are not, by them- selves, essential for success. Superior performance will usually require them to be supported in some way, however.

Organization

The middle of the scale on this trait is best for almost all types of professional sales. On one extreme are the ultra-organized. They want to plan out everything before acting. While this level of planning can be beneficial at times, too often it paralyzes sales efforts that must interact with less organized people. In telemarketing sales, inbound calls can be upsetting to highly organized salespeople, who view each un- scheduled call as an interruption.

The other extreme of the scale represents those salespeople who are purely reactive, responding to each event as it occurs. While this lack of planning makes them seem available to their customers, their loose follow up is frustrating for everyone. Retail sales situations are ideal for reactive people, since each cus- tomer interaction is unplanned. This is also ideal for inbound telemarketing sales, although any details or follow up must be supported with a field-driven software system.

Sensitivity

This is a trait that measures emotional sensitivity and how a salesperson deals with stress. Highly sensi- tive salespeople are rarely successful in professional sales. The inevitable truth about selling is that there are always more No’s than Yes’s. Highly sensitive people tend to take this type of rejection personally, and therefore they easily become discouraged. Salespeople who are in the middle of the scale tend to handle sales rejection more objectively, and those on the other extreme generally do not notice it.

In telemarketing sales, salespeople who are less sensitive are usually more comfortable in sedentary posi- tions. As salespeople become more sensitive, they require more activity. Team Motivation

Team motivation describes the competitive environment that is most motivational for a salesperson. Indi- vidually competitive salespeople thrive on individual competition. They need recognition for outstanding performance, and they want to be seen as the best by their peers. Lacking this recognition, they can be- come discouraged and demotivated despite their actual sale success. Team competitive salespeople thrive on group or team-based competition. They enjoy being part of a team without being the star of the team.

Individual recognition is not important or even motivational to them.

Case Studies of Sales Teams

Small Solution Sales

A software solutions sales company provided customized systems to large companies. Deals were typi- cally priced from $250, 000 to $1 million. Decisions involved multiple buyers throughout the organiza - tion, including CEO, CIO, CFO and other top executives. Key factors for successful salespeople were: Only fast learners could understand the complexities of this sale as it was planned. Slower learners sim- ply could not keep up with the knowledge that was necessary to drive the sale. Only highly assertive salespeople were able to control the sales process across a number of buyers within the same organiza- tion. Those salespeople who were experienced at presenting packaged solutions, and who were highly consistent lacked the flexibility to adapt their presentation to different situations with different prospects.

The sales process required multiple visits to the customer site, and those extroverted salespeople tended to develop better relationships through that interaction. This seemed to make a difference in finding champions for the new concepts.

Business - to - Consumer Sales

This was a large company that employed hundreds of salespeople to call on residential home owners, selling a technical but simple product. The salespeople fulfilled appointments that were made by a tele- marketing team based on information requests from potential customers.

An immediate discovery upon assessing a large sample of salespeople was that high general reasoning generally resulted in turnover. Again this was because the product information needed to make the sale was relatively simple, and once the training ended, the sale itself was not challenging enough to engage quicker processors on a daily basis. Interestingly, several managers with high scores in general reasoning has succeeded in the basic sales job, but had only stayed because their particular situation offered the possibility for rapid promotion. High assertiveness proved to be necessary, as the sale really needed to be closed on the initial appointment if it was to be successful. Less assertive salespeople frequently generat- ed a lot of potential sales, but the great majority of these never materialized. Mid level consistency was critical, since there was a proven sales script and sales presentation to follow. After that however, the suc- cessful salesperson was flexible enough to address the specific issues of each customer. Those that were too rigid tended to sell the concept but not its immediate value to each listener. Less consistent salespeo- ple never gained the benefit of the proven sales tools. High extroversion proved to be a plus for in-home presentation, where friendliness and enthusiasm made the prospects more comfortable. More introverted salespeople were perceived as disinterested.

Management Consulting and Training Sales

This organization of over 2000 salespeople operating in 700 franchises sold training programs and man- agement consulting services to businesses. The sale was consultative to some extent but the product and services sold were from a relatively fixed menu of options. Salespeople were responsible for finding their own prospects. Presentations were to Senior executives and business owners. These positions were large- ly commissioned jobs with no base salary.

General reasoning played an interesting part in how success was achieved by some of top performers.

While the sales process itself was straightforward and easy to learn, the potential ways of approaching businesses with this offering were limitless. One type of top performers had mid-level general reasoning and learning speed. They used the basic formula to sell 1 - 3 programs to a large number of companies.

They basically worked an activity-based sales system, mastering one way to sell and then applying that to as many prospects as possible. A second type of top producer was high in general reasoning and learned very quickly. Instead of becoming bored and leaving, they turned their efforts into more challenging ways of selling the same products. They typically sold much larger deals and often combined different types of training programs in the deals. The challenge for the company was in recognizing the value of this dual approach, and then adjusting their training and coaching to serve both types of salespeople.

High levels of assertiveness were present in almost all top producers. Salespeople with lower levels of assertiveness often transitioned from a primary selling role into one of customer service and program de- livery. A mid-level of consistency was best, allowing a salesperson to adapt to the prospect’s situation while keeping the solution within the standard menu of programs. Introverts were rarely successful. The benefits of buying training programs are conceptual rather than tangible. High extroverts had the ability to generate enthusiasm and excitement for these future benefits in their presentations, encouraging the prospect to buy.

Another critical factor appeared in this engagement. Sensitivity to stress is the principal element that af- fects the sense of urgency in a salesperson. In this case, those salespeople with a mid-level sensitivity to stress were most effective. They felt that the buying decision was a relatively quick one, and consequently, their expectations were frequently met by the prospects. More relaxed (less sensitive to stress) salespeople

Commercial Real Estate Sales

Commercial real estate sales is a high activity position that demands a great deal of time in the field with prospects and visiting buildings. There is a tremendous amount of detailed paperwork and follow up that is necessary. It is a highly regulated business with many laws and rules that must have compliance. It is also a relationship business in which building a network of contacts is essential.

Successful salespeople in commercial real estate were generally mid-level in general reasoning. While there is a great deal of information that must be acquired, it is simply a lot of the same type of informa- tion. Faster learners acquire it quickly but become bored and turnover if they cannot channel their abili- ties into creative areas of the profession. This is more likely with experience, but fast learners often do not stay long enough to gain that experience. A high level of assertiveness was necessary to move prospects through the rather long process of identifying sites and then considering a host of variables. High as - sertive salespeople were also much more successful in negotiating with landlords. Mid-level consistency was best to handle the regulations and at the same time, look for creative solutions to issues that arose.

Salespeople low in organization were unable to handle the paperwork or the details of the job. High per- formers often won deals simply by following up more efficiently than their competition. Those with low sensitivity to stress usually spent too much time in the office.

Residential Real Estate Sales

Residential real estate sales is much different than commercial real estate sales. Residential agents deal with individual home buyers rather then business owners or executives. The residential sale is much more emotional. It is less of a persuasive sale. There is detailed paperwork, but agents must often respond to many unscheduled buyer requests. There are many regulations that require compliance.

Residential real estate agents were successful with any level of general reasoning, depending upon their situation. The best ones tended to be from the mid-level group. This is because mid-level learners com- municate in a manner that is comfortable for most other people. Faster learners often outrun their listen- ers. High assertiveness continued to be important, but not in a persuasive application. The most success- ful agents used their assertiveness to guide their buyers through the decision-making process. Less as - sertive agents tended to let the buyers make their own way through the process. For experienced buyers, this worked, but with new buyers, the sale often faltered. Mid-level consistency allowed the agents to deal with the regulations and still be flexible when necessary. Clearly extroverts did far better than intro- verts, since residential sales is a very personal interaction. Any level of organization was acceptable as long as the agent managed it properly. Highly organized agents who were successful, structured their work on a more scheduled basis. Less organized agents who were successful, structured their work in such a way as to emphasize their availability at any time. Emotional sensitivity is an advantage in an emotional sale. Low sensitivity limited the agents’ ability to recognize buying signals. They also spent too much time in the office, relying on pictures and the Internet’s virtual home tours.

Summary

These case studies and many others are convincing evidence that personality traits and abilities are foundational in determining who will be successful in any professional sales role. While the critical traits may vary in different sales positions, each type of professional sales depends upon critical traits, without which sales success is unlikely.

Today, 6th and 7th Generation assessment technology can quickly and accurately measure these traits and 1 abilities in less than 30 minutes. Reports are written specifically to describe job behaviors in a way that requires no training or expert consulting to understand. Some products even provide behavioral event- based interview questions that automatically focus on each individual candidate’s issues concerning a particular job. With performance improvement as the goal of every sales manager or senior executive, it only makes sense to understand the collective capabilities of the sales team as the first step in any coach- ing effort and well before spending any money on training. Once that information is in hand, the likely success of any strategic decisions involving the sales team becomes clear. With that same information, tactical decisions, such as deployment or support requirements also become clear. Training can now be focused so as to be most effective, with the appropriate training dollars being spent on the right people.

Those salespeople who are miscast for their sales roles can then be redirected into other assignments that are more suited to their strengths.

At this point in time, assessment applications such as the Sales Team MRI™ represent a competitive ad- vantage for its users. Within a year or two, the use of such information will be necessary just to remain competitive. In three or more years, adopting these assessment applications will be an attempt to survive.

The good news is that everyone gets to pick their entry point.
