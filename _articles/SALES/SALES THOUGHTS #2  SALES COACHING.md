What Is Possible with Specific Sales Coaching

Sales Thoughts #1 identified the hard-wired behavioral traits and cognitive abilities that are necessary for sales success. If these traits and abilities are missing, they cannot be coached into existence. Too often, the sales team is presented with a smorgasbord of sales training highlighted by a motivational speaker determined to ignite sales production. Well meaning sales managers do their best to share their own experience and coach their team along the paths they know. What is needed is better and more specific DATA to guide their coaching.

If the traits and abilities are there, then coaching for improved performance is

possible. Knowing the causes of sales performance issues allows the sales manager to focus their time and efforts on the elements that will generate the greatest improvement. Key Coaching Areas for Salespeople

Ironically, two of the most common issues stem from the same strengths that drive sales success.

˒Persuasiveness - A high level of persuasiveness is absolutely a strong

advantage in selling. However, when it is pushed too far or too fast, it can

cause the prospect to back off, feeling rushed or a bit out of control.

˒Fast Information Processing - This is necessary for complex/solution

sales. The catch is that people tend to unconsciously communicate at the

same speed at which they process information. If the prospect is not

equally fast in terms of processing speed, they may not fully understand

the presentation because it is moving too fast for them. They may see the

big concept but not how it is accomplished. Visual presentations and

other aids can slow things down to a comfortable level. Asking frequent

questions to confirm the prospect’s understanding is important.

Other hard-wired personality traits affect selling, however they are much easier to support and to coach.

•Loose with Details - One third of the population is loose with details,

meaning that they must double check their work when details are

important.

•Reacts Rather Than Plans - One third of the population does little

planning, preferring to react to the situation as it happens. This can be a

strength in some jobs, but larger and more complex sales generally

require solid sales planning and preparation.

•Consistent Sales Approach - There are sales that have a proven sales

process, and success is found by repeating that process consistently. This

is not true for solution sales. Prospects may have similar needs and

situations, but the differences are significant. Salespeople who are wired

to use a consistent process can struggle with a solution sale. It helps for

them to focus their consistency on the questions in their sales interview,

letting the differences emerge from that.

•Fluid Sales Approach - Salespeople who are wired to adjust their

approach to the situation at hand have an advantage in a solution sale. In

addition, it also requires fast information processing, but with that, they

can adapt their offering to meet the different needs of each prospect.

Their challenge appears when the sale depends more on a proven

approach that is best delivered consistently with each prospect.

•Sales Cycle - Some salespeople have an intense sense of urgency. They

work best with a sale that can be closed quickly as in a single

presentation. When multiple steps are necessary, they can become

impatient, seeming to rush the prospect uncomfortably. Salespeople have

the opposite level of urgency, being patient and relaxed. This can work

successfully with a long sales cycle with the right follow up along the

way. The problem occurs when there is too much time between the

presentation which engaged the prospect and the move to a buying

decisions. Time has the unfortunate effect of blurring the buying motives

and the wonderful benefits of the purchase, leaving only the price and

•Networking - Half of the population gets energy by interacting with

other people. They look for occasions to do this, and it provides them

with opportunities for networking. With practice and skills, this gives

them prospects for sales and referrals. The other half of the population

loses energy when interacting with others. Networking is difficult for

them, and even when they make the effort, they are rarely successful.

Fortunately, there are countless ways to research and engage prospects

using the Internet. It is important for them to have a plan and track their

results with their online strategies.

•Presentation Power - About one third of the population has personality

traits that enable them to project energy and enthusiasm in their sales

presentations. This adds power to their presentations and helps to engage

the interests of their prospects. When salespeople without these

personality traits deliver the presentation, the script and visuals may be

the same, but it lacks the same energy and enthusiasm. That may work

well in a technical presentation which depends more on facts and figures.

Sometimes it works well to team a presenter with the technical person, so

that each element is covered well.

•Telling vs Selling - The salespeople that rock the presentations are

typically wonderful storytellers. That is often the strength of their sales

approach. They must be careful to allow the prospects to tell their own

story because that provides the keys to the solution sale. That solution

can only come from the prospect. Taking notes during the sales interview

is one way to ensure listening. The notes can be a valuable point of

discussion and sales planning for the sales manager.

•Balancing Company and Customer - There is a personality trait that

describes the balance between the company’s goals and the customer’s

wants. The majority of top salespeople see their personal sales success as
