## Page 1

A Pragmatic View of Sales Performance

It appears that your salespeople sell graphic media solutions of various

kinds. There are two primary critical factors for that sale. The first is high

general reasoning. The technology is evolving daily and that requires fast

learning just to keep up. More importantly, yours is a complex solution sale

that varies from client to client. Sales candidates with less than a fast

processing speed can sell products, but not solutions. In a sales interview/

presentation, they must acquire knowledge of the prospect's project, relate

that to the overall strategies of that prospect and the capabilities of your

company. All of that while taking into account the possible competitive

issues in the project, assorted creative needs, delivery challenges, and a

myriad of minor issues. Slower processors may have superb sales skills,

marvelous people skills, but they cannot handle the scope of the sales

issues. It is like a great computer that cannot handle video processing. The

second critical factor is assertiveness as a hard-wired personality trait.

Again a high level is necessary. First, it enables them to ask the hard

qualifying questions to set up the sales interview. Next, it allows the

salesperson to speak directly, confronting possible problems early on in the

process. Then it enables them to move the prospect toward a buying

decision and control the sales process. Less assertive salespeople "sell"

the deal but do not close the deal. They cannot ask the key questions so

they are constantly "surprised" by unknown elements of the project.

There are other contributing factors that impact performance, but without

these two, success in your type of sale is unlikely. These are easily

measured with current technology, in less than thirty minutes and

inexpensively without consultants or experts. If you check these things

before getting into sales skills and other selection issues, your percentage

will reverse.

## Page 2

I just love conceptual debates. It is a grand flurry of opinions, all with merit, but dependent upon someone grasping the elements and then finding a way to implement them in the middle of their normal business. Twenty years of solving such issues have taught me a reverence for pragmatic solutions. A simple online survey can exclude job candidates who CANNOT deliver a sale as needed. What is left is a field of candidates who can sell acceptably to great, regardless of how well the concepts are implemented.

Technology changed the way we get information and how we

communicate. It has also changed the way we find talent.

The psychometric world has been a blind area for far too long. Obsolete

theories and outdated products survive in a murky place where customers

only see what comes out of the laser printer. It looks new and the report

reads well. Their use makes hiring better than it was without them.
