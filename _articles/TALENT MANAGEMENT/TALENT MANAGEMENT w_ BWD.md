Strengths Inventories of Work Groups

Every business has a Talent Warehouse, their human resources. The

talents in that warehouse represent the human capital of the enterprise.

The ability of the business to effectively use the potential of that human

capital is directly related to how well they understand the inventory of job

competencies in each work group. Skills and experience have proven to be

dangerously inadequate for this. The fact that a person played baseball in

the majors for ten years is dramatically different in value to a record

showing an ERA of 1.8 and a won-loss record of 18-4 for the Red Sox. As

information becomes more specific, it enables more effective business

decisions.

BestWork DATA produces exactly that level of information on the job

capabilities of each individual. When that information is collected and

compiled into a composite of the job competencies of the work group, it is

possible to get a clear picture of what the team’s actual capabilities are and

more importantly, where there are weakness or potential issues to

executing the current strategies.

Sales Team Analysis

A software company launched a new offering which required a complex,

solution-oriented sale. They recruited salespeople with a track record of

success in software sales and who were highly competitive, evidenced by

awards or varsity sports participation in college. The training was intensive

and thorough. The products were sound and offered significant value that

was documented and demonstrable. Yet, for over two years, the team

struggled without meeting quota. Management and several consultants

reworked the sales pitch and intensified the marketing, with little results.

Complex or strategic sales typically require fast information processing in

and the leader were simply not equipped for this new sale. No amount of

training or consulting was going to compensate for this mismatch of

capabilities and sales strategy.

seemed to take forever, and with few exceptions, there was no apparent

sales team is on the slower side of the bell curve, and 50% of the team plus

the Sales Manager are in the 25th percentile in terms of urgency. That

group accepts a slower sales cycle and sees no need to push their

prospects to move the sale forward. There is no training or coaching that

will change that core trait, but knowing the specific cause of longer sales

cycles suggests using a different management strategy with more frequent

milestones. It suggests that a stepped sales process may help to move the

decision in a more predictable manner.

Executive Team Analysis

This company pioneered a space within the manufacturing industry, quickly

growing to 100 million in revenue. Seven years later, they found

themselves #3 in the industry and on their way to #4, despite strong

margins, excellent customer reviews, high quality, and on time

performance. The CEO, President, CFO, and VP of Sales completed the

BestWork DATA survey, and the results from their individual scores were

compiled into this Executive Strengths Inventory. These four scales each

display one of the four clues to the cause of the company’s decline in

market share.

A - Strategic vs. Tactical Thinking

First, some executives tend to focus their attention and energies on tactical

or short term issues, those impacting the current quarter and perhaps the

following one. In fact, this company was excelling in tactical performance: profit margins, customer service, and quality products. B - Challenging & Debating Ideas

All four of the top executive were highly assertive, quick to state their

opinions and to confront any challenges to that opinion. There is a lot of

strength in this type of management team, but defending opinions is not the

same things as discussing and sharing ideas. It requires clear knowledge

of how each person is wired, supported by a communication structure that

channels the confrontations into collaboration for this kind of team to really

prosper without squandering their energy with internal bickering. The fourth

trait scale of Team (D) compounded this problem. Each of the executives

was individually-oriented. In other words, they wanted to excel, to win, to be

the best, and to be right. They did not want to fail, to lose, to not be the

best, or to be wrong. The company was faced with losing market share and

each one wanted to know why and each one wanted to avoid the blame.

With their strengths of assertiveness, they fought to be right and defended

their actions. The energy of the company was turned away from the

marketplace and spent on these internal blame-storming sessions.

C - Innovation

This scale is the principal driver of innovation. In effect, as the score moves

lower, the individual moves further away from conventional thinking. Here

all four executives were in the conventional half of the population. They

kept getting better at essentially doing the same thing that they had been

doing. Meanwhile, their competition had been leapfrogging them in

innovations, thus taking market share.

The power of this level of assessment information is found in the objective

products. The problem was that their level of “strategic” thinking was

focused on the short term of the business. Their level of “innovation” was

Supervisory Study

This case involved the head teller of a bank and the seven tellers reporting

to her. Banks are generally successful at hiring detail-oriented tellers who

tend to follow rules. This is partly because people who are not detail-

oriented and who do not like convention and regulations do not tend to

apply for jobs in banking. The head teller had been an exceptional teller,

and that performance had resulted in her promotion to the supervisory role.

Unfortunately, in that role her performance had been unsatisfactory. There

were tardiness issues among her team, and morale was poor, which was

sometimes reflected in their customer interactions.

In this BestWork DATA Work Group Strengths Inventory, the distribution of

the teller team is shown on three scales, with the head teller’s score

indicated by the underscore. As would be expected, the head teller and her

reports all clustered at the end for Highly Detailed Work and for

Following Rules & Procedures. She was at the extreme end of both

scales, and therefore, she was extremely picky about how things were to

be done. The source of the problem becomes clear when looking at the

third scale which address an individual’s ability to Direct the Actions of

Others.

The job of a supervisor is filled with confrontation. They must give feedback

on the performance of the employees in order for the employees to improve

that performance. The feedback must be specific. “Try to do better” or “This
