Not That Major…This Major - A Student Story

Stories are one of the best ways to explain things. About four years ago,

one of our BestWork DATA clients asked me if I could help his son, a

freshman in college. The son had an exemplary record in high school with

excellent grades. He was fascinated with architecture, and that career

choice was strong and clear when he elected to attend a university with an

exceptional architectural program. Surprisingly, in his freshman year, he

struggled with his first year architectural classes. His grades were poor,

nothing like what he had achieved in high school. His study habits were

good, and there were no contributing factors that could explain the

problem. Counseling at the university was not helpful, and his parents,

while absolutely supportive, seemed to make no difference.

His father called me, asking if his son could experience BestWork, as it was

a key to the talent acquisition and talent management at his company. I

said that not only was it a great idea, but that we actually had a complete

program designed for students. A few weeks went by without hearing from

either the father or the son. I called and his dad explained that his son was

working with a psychologist because the situation had led the son into

depression. When that improved, they would do BestWork. After several

weeks, the father invited me to lunch, still searching for some way to help

his son. At last, his son completed the questionnaire and I had his DATA,

the measurement of his hard-wired personality traits and cognitive

abilities.

His speed of learning and processing information was at the highest level,

which explained his success in high school. He was precise and

conventional with his thinking. He valued consistency with process and

procedures, and wanted a proven path to follow in his work. That would

match perfectly with the classic architectural program found in many

universities. Unfortunately, the university which he had chosen emphasized

graduated with a degree in mechanical engineering. Thanks!” He now

wants his other son to take BestWork before college.

This story is important because it is a powerful example of how an

intelligent student with clear direction can still find themselves in a

mismatched major. DATA reveals a student’s hard-wired strengths and

abilities in a way that everyone can understand, both parents and students.
