## Page 1

- Exploring the Promising & the Probable

I recently listened to a college guidance counselor promote the importance

of having students “explore and experience” a variety of subjects during

their college experience. She described college as an “adventure in

learning” and in her life, the things she least expected had led her to an

amazing career. Not surprisingly, she cautioned against “too much

direction” when advising a student about majors or even colleges. There is

no doubt that “too much” of anything is by definition, not good. On the other

hand, not enough of the right thing can also be a problem.

Students and this guidance counselor share a common personality trait,

and one which is hard-wired, meaning that it is a stable foundation for their

behaviors and how they learn. On one end of the normal bell curve are

individuals who are wide open to new things. They love variety and crave

change. On the other end of that bell curve are individuals who prefer to

focus on one thing, learning more and more about it. People tend to give

advice that matches how they are wired. This guidance counselor was

clearly in the first group, thriving on variety. Her advice sounded great for

students just like her. It was less welcome for those on the other end of the

curve.

The catch is that each group has the challenge of choosing and finishing

some course of study that will connect with a job. Celebrate Strengths has

tools such as Try On Jobs (www.tryonjobs.com) that allow students to

explore jobs in the way that is best for them. Try On Jobs also alerts

students when a particular job choice is not a good match for their

strengths.

Students in general, are undecided about career direction and therefore,

are undecided about college majors. Too often, indecision and lack of

knowledge become the drivers for “exploration”. Try On Jobs directs that

exploration into areas that are the most promising and the most probable.
