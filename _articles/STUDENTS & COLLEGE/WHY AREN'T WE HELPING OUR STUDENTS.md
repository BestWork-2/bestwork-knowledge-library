A Trillion Dollar Debt & Iffy Jobs

Why Aren’t We Helping Our Students?

Imagine your son or daughter asking for a phone as

they leave for college in 2020. You are a loving and

supportive parent with the means to provide the

phone so you give them the phone shown here. This

phone was made the same year as the interests

surveys that most guidance counselors used to help

your son or daughter make decisions about their

college majors and which college they should attend.

They will probably be writing a lot of papers in

college, so send them away with the leading

technology of that same time period, the IBM

Selectric. Remember that this is the same level of

technology that influence your child’s college

decisions, the multi-thousand dollar decisions that

will affect their future job

opportunities.

If by some chance, their guidance counselors favored

Myers-Briggs for insights, then this is what students

from that time frame used for writing papers. If

typewriter technology changed so much, did

psychology and psychometrics just stand still? By the

way, they are out of luck getting a mobile phone from

that era.

Please don’t tell me they used some version of DISC to help

your child. That model of personality is over 2000 years old, and

was suggested by Hippocrates. The words groups that make up

the instrument were developed by William Marston in 1928.

Probably nothing else in the school is as old as that.

If the counselors were hitching their guidance star to the government’s O

NET system, it might be prudent to note that the O NET system was never

designed to connect students, graduates or anyone else with the likelihood

of job success. O NET’s mission is to provide infinitely detailed job

Retail Sales Workers, Energy Brokers) to be used for Department of Labor

The mystery of why schools and guidance counselors persist in using such

inadequate tools to help students, who are presumably the future of the

country, make such important decisions is indeed puzzling.

•Poorly chosen majors have resulted in an average rate of over three

changes of major per student.

•Almost 40% of students who start college never graduate with

degrees.

•Six years is the current measurement standard for getting a four year

degree.

•Unemployed and underemployment rates for college graduates is at

an all time high.

•Total student loan debt is approaching one trillion dollars.

•Over 30% of the student debt is past due or under forbearance,

accumulating crushing interest.

These are only a tiny selection of the frightening statistics that characterize

the college students’ plight in the United States. This calamity is not limited

to impersonal statistics. Almost everyone knows someone whose son or

daughter is either undecided about a career direction, unhappy with their

choice, struggling to find a job, “lacking motivation” or is in some other way

not positively engaged with their education or their career.

The career counselor ranks are unfortunately not lacking in those who

would profit from this situation. One family spent three thousand dollars for

a professional college counselor to assist their daughter in selecting the

right college. This “professional” rehashed a collection of simple and

outdated assessments to compile a report recommending that she attend

the state college and major in psychology. Four years later, she is

indifferent to psychology and has no idea of what to do now. Her story or a

similar version could be told of tens of thousands of students.

Every day the Wall Street Journal features one or more stories focused on

the latest brick to fall from the crumbling edifice of college education in the

USA. It might be about students selecting majors that do not lead to sound

The world of business has been rocked over the past few years with

breakthrough technologies for understanding and managing human capital.

Thought-leading companies that have embraced these new solutions now

find making bad hires a thing of the past. Performance problems are easily

resolved with DATA that correlates the employees’ strengths and abilities

with specific job behaviors needed for success. Just as sports teams have

achieved their highest levels of performance through increased

performance data on their players, so too have businesses seeing levels of

performance never before imagined through the use of people DATA.

Today it is easy to accurately and reliably measure hard-wired traits and

abilities. These traits and abilities are foundational to any kind of thinking,

learning or behaving. These characteristics, as measured today, do not

change over time or with coaching or training. They form a solid foundation

upon which to build a career, or they form a solid set of obstacles to

success, depending upon whether or not they match the demands of the

specific type of job.

Imagine this DATA being applied to the decisions being made by students

as they consider a world of majors and college choices.

•If a student does not handle details well, is accounting a sound career

choice?

•If a student is creative, never following the rules but seeking the

exceptions, is engineering going to be their best fit?

•If a student dislikes confrontation of any kind, does pursuing

entrepreneurship make sense?

These are examples of actual decisions made by students with the “help” of

counselors using old assessment tools. They would never have been made

with new DATA.

Education has a stated goal of preparing young people for successful

careers. If that is to be more than the weak and unfulfilled promise that it is

today, guidance programs for students has to move into this century. The
