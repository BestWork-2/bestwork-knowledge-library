Doing What You Are Good At Doing

A Much Clearer Path for Students

“What is your passion?” “What are you interested in?” “What do you love?”

These questions and more like them have been asked of students for

decades. They have been asked by parents, teachers, guidance

counselors, and by well meaning friends and neighbors. They have been

asked and expounded upon by popular books, popular media, and by

society at large. Outdated assessments and countless online

questionnaires have offered worlds of advice on college majors and career

directions to parents and students desperate to find some meaningful help

in making these hugely expensive and far-reaching decisions. Despite the

sincerity of these efforts and despite the students’ determination to answer,

a perusal of recent media reports shows a shocking contrast between the

promise and the reality.

•The average college student changes majors three times.

•Only about 58% of students that start college, actually graduate.

•60% of college students take longer than 4 years to graduate.

•6 years is considered normal in many government statistics.

•The total student debt is over one trillion dollars.

•Many graduates cannot find a job related to their college degree.

The principal reason for changing majors is either a lack of interest in the

major or poor performance in those classes. Either of these lead to more

years and more costs or unfortunately, failure to finish. Obviously, the

current methods are not working. They are not “time-tested”. They just have

not been working for a long time and no one seemed to notice. A tighter

economy brings the problems into high relief. The costs have become

unbearable for many. The tough job market no longer hires “degrees”.

Companies seek performance. For too long, degrees have not guaranteed

that or even been a strong indicator of it. More than half of all companies

use some form of testing or assessments to make hiring decisions. The

quality of these tools is improving every day. It no longer matters what the

This would seem to be common sense and intelligent people recognize the

practical truth of this. Why then, are so many students uncertain or just

plain misdirected when it comes to choosing a course of study. There are

three main reasons. One is inherent to the situation. Another is caused by

well meant but flawed encouragement. The last is a travesty caused by

poor information provided by sadly outdated tools promoted by profit-

seeking enterprises or by sincere guidance counselors who are simply

unaware of the limitations of the tools they use.

Deciding a career direction at any stage of life is challenging for most

people. Even after years of experience, over half of the working population

questions whether or not they are in the right job. Students are faced with

the same challenge but lack the years of experience. This is the inherent

problem. Students are generally only slightly familiar with a tiny spectrum of

occupations that exist in their world. Even that knowledge is usually limited

or colored by a handful of events that made a certain career path stand out.

Still parents, educators and even state governments push for students to

make career choices as soon as possible, some even before high school.

Too often, once that course is set in their mind, it is blindly pursued in the

hopes that everything will work out in the end. That is the second problem.

There is an obvious bias in this for well known professions, such as doctor,

lawyer, teacher, engineer, accountant, actor or others. Approximately half of

The third problem is that schools and guidance counselors use archaic

instruments and simplistic career surveys to provide direction for students.

People who would not be caught dead using a 2-year old cell phone

routinely use products based on fifty year old thinking and long-abandoned

by the leading scientists and thinkers of that world to guide students in

need of real help. The Internet has proven to be a fertile ground for both

sincere but uninformed entrepreneurs and a host of charlatans who

promise much and offer little in the way of meaningful direction to students

and parents. Some of these offer free tests with value commensurate with

the cost. The problem is that often the information, however poorly derived,

is taken to heart by sincere students. Unfortunately, it is just “right” enough

to be dangerously “wrong”, sending the students off on an educational

goose chase.

The amazing thing is that none of this is necessary. For decades, leading

psychologists have known that human beings have hard-wired personality

traits. These are really well established by the age of twelve, and they form

the foundation of each person’s unique strengths. It is the basis of all

behavior. It is why some things come easily and why other things are much

harder. For example, the ability to work with detailed information, such as is

required for most financial or technical subjects, depends upon a hard-

wired personality traits. Approximately one third of the population does this

easily; another third can do it, but it takes more effort, energy and

concentration; and the last third struggles with it. Similarly, human beings

have cognitive abilities that are the foundation of how they think and learn.

Some people learn quickly but get bored with routine tasks. Others learn

more slowly but can master certain kinds of information. Different cognitive

abilities are required for different majors just as different ones are required

for different career paths.

The question arises, “Isn’t it possible for anyone to do anything?”

Absolutely! The glitch is that when a student’s traits and abilities do not

match the ones needed for a particular course of study or a particular job,

they must put substantially more time and energy into that course or that

job. Unfortunately, even with that additional time and energy, they will rarely

achieve more than a merely adequate level of success. On the other hand,

when the course of study or the particular job matches their core strengths

and abilities, they can perform with minimal time and energy, and with a bit

of extra effort, they can excel.

It would seem to make sense then, that knowing a student’s hard-wired

traits and abilities would be a sound first step toward considering various

career options and college choices. The good news is that technology

makes that quite simple. The Students and College division of Celebrate

Strengths has created tools specifically designed for this purpose. They

require no experts and are easily understood by any student or parent. It is

based on a 25 minute online experience, which uses the same instruments

that many businesses use in their selection process. That DATA is then

used to produce a series of reports to help make better decisions about

majors, colleges, friends, and more. Even better, it costs less than those

tennis or piano lessons or last month’s cell phone bill.
