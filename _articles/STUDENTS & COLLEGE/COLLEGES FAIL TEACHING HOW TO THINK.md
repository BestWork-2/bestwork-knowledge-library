Many Colleges Fail in Teaching How to Think

Excerpt from the Wall Street Journal June 6, 2017

Freshmen and seniors at about 200 colleges across the USA take a little-

known test every year to measure how much better they get at learning

how to think. The results are discouraging.

At more than half of the schools, at least a third of the seniors were unable

to make a cohesive argument, assess the quality of evidence in a

document or interpret data in a table.

At some of the most prestigious flagship universities, test results indicate

the average graduate shows little or no improvement in critical thinking over

four years.

A survey by Payscale Inc. showed that 50% of employers complain that the

college graduates they hire are not ready for the workplace. The #1

complaint…Poor critical reasoning skills.

International ranking show US college graduates are in the middle of the

pack for numeracy and literacy and near the bottom when it comes to

problem solving.

Note: General reasoning, as measured by our DATA, is the primary factor

in critical thinking and problem solving. Our DATA unbundles the specific

strengths of people so that their unique strengths can be optimized.

Without that knowledge, all students are bundled together. Dramatically

different capabilities are addressed in one uniform way. It is no wonder that

it is not effective.

Imagine organizing a car race with an assortment of cars, ranging from

stock cars, Indy cars, Gran Prix cars, drag racers and some garden variety

hot rods. Some start fast and finish quickly. Some start slowly and go fast

for hours. Some are best in straight lines and some are designed for

curves. Putting those cars into one race is what happens with students in

our schools. No one optimizes their strengths because of the unintended
