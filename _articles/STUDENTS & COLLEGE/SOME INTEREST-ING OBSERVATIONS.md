Some Interest-ing Observations

For over twenty years, I have been an authority on the business applications of

psychometric testing. I recently attended a national conference on career

counseling, both in high schools and colleges, and of course, I was curious as to what tools were commonly used in that world. To say that I was shocked is an understatement. The show was attended by sincere professionals with a true passion for helping students make better decisions. However, they had little or no knowledge about newer generations of assessment technology. In fact, the vast majority of attendees were using tools that have long been outdated in the business world. I suggest that the following observations can serve as a positive motivator for exploring more advanced alternatives.

For eight decades, The Strong Interest Inventory has “been a standard bearer

in the field of vocational and educational interest measurement.” They claim that more than 70% of colleges and universities use it to assist students in identifying their educational and vocational interests.

•Is the fact that it is 80+ years old, updated but still based on the

thinking from the 1920’s, a good thing? Has psychology stood still on

that subject or has it evolved dramatically as other sciences have

done? There were no computers, no Internet, no television, and World

War II was years away. The jobs have been changed, but the

foundational concept is the same. Is it still the best thinking that we

can offer our children?

•Student debt is over $ 1 Trillion. A major cause of is that the average

college student changes majors more than twice. Each change is

expensive for both the student and the college.

•If 70% of the colleges are using the Strong Interest Inventory to guide

the students’ choices, does it sound like it is working?

•Over half of the working population in the United States are not happy

in their jobs.

•Psychological tests refer to validity and the research definitely shows

that the SII is valid. The unfortunate fact is that validity is not

necessarily connected with effectiveness. To be more specific, the

Strong Interest Inventory is just not effective enough for what today’s

students need.

•If the Strong is the “standard bearer,” perhaps it is time to pass the

flag.
