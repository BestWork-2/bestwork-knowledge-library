Thoughts on Elements of College Admission Process

There are many alternatives for collecting DATA and information which can

be used to select the students that are most likely to thrive as part of the

college student body. Here are my thoughts on the positive and negative

aspects of each alternative.

It is helpful to consider these alternatives in the same way as successful

commercial businesses view the recruiting and selection of employees.

Similarly, those businesses wish to secure employees that will perform

successfully, contributing to the overall success of the enterprise. At the

same time, they wish to avoid candidates that are likely to struggle in their

jobs or finding the jobs unsatisfactory, choose to leave the company

prematurely. Experience has shown that the simplest and most effective

practice is to first screen out those candidates whose strengths and abilities

do not fit with the strengths and abilities necessary for a particular job.

Once that is done, it becomes far easier to identify those candidates whose

strengths, abilities, experience and other attributes can best fit that role.

Students & College DATA - A 25 minutes online experience accurately

measures an applicant’s hard-wired strengths and cognitive abilities. That

DATA can then be used to evaluate how that student will think, learn and

behave within the college student body, including academic and social

situations.

Advantages

•Accurate and reliable DATA

•Easy to understand

•Can set clear screening factors to filter high volume

•Inexpensive (about 50¢ per applicant)

•DATA shows both positive and negatives.

•The same DATA guides student through entire academic career.

Negatives

•New technology requires rethinking of traditional models

Grades History - Grades show the student’s academic performance in

high school. The relevance and reliability of those grades depends upon

the rigor and quality of the high school.

Advantages

•Grades are directly related to academic performance, and

therefore poor grades are a reliable indicator of poor performance

at the college level.

•Can connect specific courses to prospective majors

Negatives

•The lack of knowledge about the rigor and quality of high school

makes the relative value of good grades to be uncertain with

regard to college performance.

SAT/ACT Scores - Specialized tutoring and test accommodations have

rendered the reliability of these tests to be uncertain. Low scores tend to be

reliable indicators but high scores may be false indicators.

Advantages

•Low scores are reliable indicators of poor or challenging

performance.

•Specialized elements can indicate problems with prospective

majors.

Negatives

•High and satisfactory scores are no longer reliable indicators of

successful academic performance at the suggested level.

•SAT/ACT scores have never been indicators of specific academic

or social elements that are key to overall success at the college.

Essays - Essays can be a clear demonstration of a student’s writing ability,

although there are multiple variables that often apply to the writer of the

essay. Did that person actually write it? How much help did they get?

Advantages

•A poor essay is a reliable indicator of poor or challenging

performance.

Negatives

•A good essay is not a reliable indicator of successful academic

performance.

Personal Interview - Personal interviews enable the college to see how

the student communicates in person and how they present themselves.

Advantages

•The nature of interviews allows the college to connect on a more

personal level with students they wish to enroll.

Negatives

•A good personal interview means that the student connected with

the interviewer and that they interview well. In no way does it

reliably indicate their ability to perform well academically in

college.

•A poor personal interview means that the student did not connect

with the interviewer and/or they do not interview well. In no way

does it reliably indicate anything about their ability to perform

academically in college.

Video Interview - Video interviews enable the college to see how the

student communicates and how they handle improvisational thinking,

depending on their level of comfort being on camera.

Advantages

•The video interview shows how the student communicates.

•Next to a personal interview, a video interview allow the student to

connect more closely with the college.

Negatives

•A good video interview means that the person is good at

interviewing and answering questions extemporaneously, but it

does not reliably indicate the probability of their success

academically in college.

•A poor video interview means that the person is not good at

interviewing or at answering questions extemporaneously, but it

does not reliably indicate the probability of their success

academically in college.

Phone or Skype Interview - A scheduled conversation by phone or Skype

enables the college to see how the student communicates. A well-

formulated interview model would enhance the effectiveness of this model.

Advantages

•The phone/Skype interview shows how the student

communicates.

•The interview allows the student to connect more closely with the

college.

Negatives

•A good phone/Skype interview means that the person is good at

interviewing and answering questions extemporaneously, but it

does not reliably indicate the probability of their success

academically in college.

•A poor phone/Skype interview means that the person is not good

at interviewing or at answering questions extemporaneously, but it

does not reliably indicate the probability of their success

academically in college.

NOTE: DATA from the Students & College experience can be used to

target the interview questions specifically for each student, focusing

attention on the key issues that student may have in college.

Portfolio Review - Some schools are having students compile a portfolio

throughout their high school career, including examples of their best work,

essays on what they are proud of, extracurricular activities and other

things. This has been a standard for many arts programs, and this is an

attempt to apply that to a wider range of majors.

Advantages

•This offers a wider view of the student’s work.

•The very compilation of the portfolio is an indication of the

student’s ability to work on a project over an extended period of

time.

Negatives

•There is still the possibility of “padding” the portfolio.

•It clearly has a stronger relationship with some majors than

others.

•There is no standard among schools for portfolios and many

schools do not use this program.

Interest Survey Information - College guidance counselors and some

schools use traditional interest surveys to support college and major

choices for their students.

Advantages

•There are none.

Negatives

•Unfortunately, interest surveys have little to do with a student’s

ability to fit a particular college or a particular major. All too often

they support the idea or interest of the moment.

Recommendations: There are three distinct objectives when these various elements are examined.

Screening - Eliminating undesirable applicants (NO’S)

Selection - Identifying those applicants who are most desirable

(STARS) first and then the best applicants from the rest (OK’s).

Recruiting - Engaging the most desirable applicants (STARS) to

persuade them to choose this college
