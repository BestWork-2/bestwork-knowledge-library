The Wrong Model Cannot Lead You to the Right Solution

The traditional model of the relationship between people and jobs is shown in

Illustration A.

Illustration A

Students were asked directly or with various survey instruments, “What careers

are you interested in pursuing?” Their answers were generally based on little

practical knowledge or experience about the actual jobs. Job seekers focused

those jobs available in their area. Down-sized workers or those looking for

employment through government programs were directed to whatever training

programs were available. If the person had some experience or skills, they

followed a similar course for their next job.

Today, there is a clearer and far more accurate model, proven through

thousands of cases and years of research, shown in Illustration B.

Illustration B

hiring, training and management. It revealed potentials in people that had

never been seen before. It also showed why smart, hard working people

sometimes failed at their jobs or struggled with new jobs. It turns out that

when job training was successful, those individuals possessed the

strengths and abilities necessary to use the knowledge and skills learned

in the job training. When the job training was unsuccessful, those

individuals had often acquired the knowledge and skills, but they lacked

the necessary strengths and abilities needed to perform that particular job.

There were many other jobs that they could have done, but the training for

them was misdirected. It was not a question of wanting to do the job or of

being interested in doing the job. It was not a question of skills or knowing

how. DATA answers the question of whether the individual CAN or

CANNOT do the job. See Illustration C.

Illustration C

There are a limited number of jobs that a person cannot do. There are an infinite number of job that a person can do. When a person is counseled into the wrong job; assigned training for the wrong job; or promoted into the wrong job, the best they can do is to struggle to just be adequate in that job. It is almost impossible for them to excel. Now the next course of their employment is affected by that performance. Potential employers see a mediocre job history.

The person does not feel successful, and this affects everything in their life.

Simply looking at the DATA prevents this. It opens up a world of choices for

people who can now understand their core strengths and abilities as being

separate from a specific set of experience or skills. Knowledge and skills can

always be acquired. It is the hard-wired traits and cognitive abilities that can be the foundation of new jobs.
