These are some of the college-specific engagements involving BestWork : 1.The United States Naval Academy The United States Naval Academy was seeking a way to resolve several key elements in their selection process.

The Academy reviewed academic histories and SAT/ACT scores. If those

were adequate, the Blue & Gold officers, retired naval officers, interviewed

the candidates. By law, the Academy must have representation from all

Congressional districts. This had proven to be dif ficult because of the

disparity in schools within some of the districts. The SAT/ACT scores were

not high enough simply because the quality of schools and the social

situation of many students presented challenges to their education.

Interviewing gave the students insights into the Navy and what would be

expected at the Academy. There was value in the interview process, but too

much of it relied on the opinions of the Blue & Gold officers, which did not

directly address the problem.

After reviewing the existing process, we made the following

recommendations : A.BestWork’s cognitive instrument measures general reasoning and fluid intelligence. This provides an accurate and reliable evaluation of the capability of a candidate to handle the rigorous academics of the Academy, regardless of the quality of their previous education. - Notre Dame

Discussions or Lectures

Notre Dame classes are centered around active discussion more

than lectures. The problem was incoming students being unable or

unwilling to participate in class discussions. While there are several

factors that may influence a student’s lack of participation, the

primary one is a hard-wired personality trait which determines an

individual’s level of comfort in speaking up in a group to challenge

ideas or debate issues. Depending upon the composition of the

discussion group, this would affect a third to one half of the normal

population. Identifying this early in their college application process

can easily direct them to an institution which favored a lecturing

model.

A Thirst for Knowledge, Creative Thinking & Leadership

Over the years, Notre Dame had crafted an inspirational description

of the ideal Notre Dame student. The characteristics described were

important and certainly desirable by anyone. However, as important

and desirable they were, there seemed to be no way to discern the

degree to which any prospective student possessed them. BestWork

applied the models of behavioral and cognitive DATA to this question

with marvelous results as illustrated here : •A Thirst for Knowledge - People learn at different speeds. This is a more accurate description of cognitive ability than the common notion of “smart.” The valedictorian of a class might learn slowly or quickly with the only difference being how much time they spent studying. •Creative Thinking - Creativity is the ability to conceive of new

ideas, new ways of doing things, alternatives to the way things

have been done. Creativity is seen in the arts, in engineering, in

medical research and in education. Creative thinking depends

upon several factors but the primary one is a hard-wired

personality trait that determines how open an individual is to

different ideas and ways of doing things. Other elements such as

experience, knowledge and willingness to challenge existing ideas

impact a person’s perceived creativity, but the foundation is the

key and that is the personality trait. One third of the student

population is comfortable with how things are done now and how

they have been done. Change and accepting new ideas and ways

of doing things is difficult for them. At the other end of a bell curve,
