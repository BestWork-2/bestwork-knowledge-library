Understanding Seeming Contradictions with DATA

Occasionally, a new user of BestWork DATA appears to see a contradiction

between the DATA report and what they have observed with a particular

individual. This is a normal experience, generally based on a degree of

skepticism with a radically new set of information. Sometimes a new user’s

first instinct is to see if the DATA report is “accurate.” Naturally, this is done by comparing past observations and experience with the report, but this is a bit misleading. BestWork DATA uses 7th Generation psychometric instruments which are the most accurate and reliable in the industry. They provide insights into the foundational drivers of behavior that cannot be seen or assessed through experience or interaction. In other words, DATA is accessing a deeper level of information that shows things not seen by other methods. Similarly, a physician may conduct an external examination of a patient and draw preliminary conclusions. However, an MRI or CAT scan gives them a much deeper and more accurate understanding of what is causing the external issues. Just as with BestWork DATA reports, the MRI or CAT scan information may confirm, deny or modify the doctor’s earlier conclusions.

In order to get the greatest value from BestWork DATA, there are a few

important things to remember.

BestWork DATA’s reports have been proven to be accurate and

reliable through extensive statistical analysis. Their value is a solid

reference point by which you can better understand the observed or

desired behaviors in individuals. The DATA is the proven reference point,

not the observed behavior. An individual not feeling well may visit a doctor,

who may give them an initial blood test to screen for bacterial infections. If

the test comes back negative, it was not a failure, but an indication that

something other than bacteria are causing the problem. If the BestWork

DATA does not seem to match the observation, there are other factors

affecting the situation.

The individual created the BestWork DATA report. At its heart, the

BestWork DATA experience simply provides a means for an individual to

describe themselves, frankly and openly, in a remarkably intimate manner.

They are asked to make a series of micro choices which are essentially the

same type of choices they make in their lives every day. These micro

Observation is questionable. When the police question witnesses, all of

whom had the same perspective on an incident, it is rare for any two

witnesses to report the same observations. A primary reason for this is that

a person’s observations of another individual’s behavior are seen through

the lens of their own behavior in similar situations. A simple example: A six

footer looks tall to a five footer but not to a seven footer. Non-assertive

people view almost everyone else as being assertive, direct or pushy,

depending upon the situation. Highly assertive people would describe the

same people as quiet, passive or pushovers in the same situations. DATA

translates behaviors into a common frame of reference so that different

people can discuss behaviors knowing that they share the same definitions

of behavior.

A person can exhibit a different behavior but there is a cost.

Observation of another person’s behavior is never constant. It is one or

more occurrences that were notable for some reason. It is possible for an

individual to exhibit almost any behavior, regardless of what their core

personality traits are, but not for an extended period of time. DATA shows

what they can deliver easily and without conscious effort. It is what comes

naturally, and those behaviors are always available. When a different level

of behavior is required, there is a cost in stress and energy. The amount of

stress and energy is directly related to the gap between what is needed

and what is naturally available. If it is for a short period of time or for an

isolated event, the person may be able to pay the necessary price in stress

and energy. When the demand becomes too extreme or too extended, the

cost becomes unbearable. This leads to poor job performance and

turnover. DATA makes early identifications of such gaps possible.

DATA showed that he would struggle with closing sales, but his

production was good. How was this possible? John’s office was in a

small town, a town in which his grandfather had been the mayor,

Example B: Bob sold life insurance for a major company, and his

Example C: A supervisor of a manufacturing operation excelled at

that job, with both outstanding production and high quality in the

products. DATA warned that the supervisor was not particularly

assertive and may be challenged by managing the actions of others.

What was his secret? DATA also showed that the workers on the

manufacturing line were generally less assertive than the supervisor.

He was comfortable managing that work team. A more assertive

group of workers would have certainly caused him problems.

Anyone can lead or manage as long as they are leading or

managing the right group for them.

Words have many interpretations. While BestWork DATA reports are

written in clear and easily understood language, they still use words that

can be interpreted many ways. Sometimes a particular word may have a

value context, either positive or negative, that impacts the way in which it is

understood. Seeking to influence productivity, companies often foster

concepts such as teamwork, culture, leadership, collaboration,

communication and more. These words and the accompanying

terminology, while laudable and inspirational, represent an amalgam of

behaviors, attitudes, values, experience and skills. DATA reveals the

foundational traits and abilities that are necessary to implement the job

behaviors that are then described by those conceptual terms. DATA is a

deeper level of specificity, and it is at a level that connects directly with

actions and decisions.
