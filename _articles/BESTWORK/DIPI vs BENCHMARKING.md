DATA, DIPI & Better Decision-Making

BestWork DATA uses an advanced reporting system based on Direct

Performance Information (DIPI). Rather than look at how an individual

matches an artificial model, benchmark or profile of the top performers in a

job, DIPI describes how that individual would perform the Critical and

Important job behaviors needed in that job. Reports using benchmarking

offer a percentage match to the benchmark (Candidate A - 84%, Candidate

B - 65%, and Candidate C - 78%). The obvious conclusion is that A is the

best choice with C being the next best. In most cases, B would be

unsatisfactory, having failed to meet the 70% threshold. It is difficult, if not

impossible to discern exactly what the differences are among the

candidates. How important is the missing % in each case. Unfortunately,

the text available with most products using this methodology is generalized

or even contradictory to such an extent that the users depend mainly on the

percentage number. This allows strong matches that lack key factors to slip

through, and weaker matches who have the key factors but lack less

important elements to be excluded. Compounding the problem, the

generalized text is of so little use that the hiring decision is the last time the information on the person’s performance is used.

BestWork DATA describes the actual job behaviors of each individual in

language that anyone can understand, as in the examples below: •Persuasive Sales Job Report - Has considerable difficulty persuading prospects to take action; Cannot close persuasive sales •Management Job Report - Feedback given can be less direct and less specific when it is negative, limiting the ability of the person to adjust their behavior effectively •IT Position - Misses details in writing code; Projects often require much debugging © Chuck Russell 2018 All Rights Reserved

There are 10 - 15 statements of this type describing each job behavior in

any single job. The Job Report provides a complete description of how that

individual will perform those behaviors. This level of specificity allows the

recruiter to screen out candidates that clearly cannot deliver any of the

Critical job behaviors necessary for that job.

The DATA is also available in a chart showing the job behaviors with the

candidate’s scores displayed on a normal distribution. This makes it easy to

compare candidates and even to match candidates with the most effective

manager or supervisor. Of course, behavioral event-based interview

questions are provided for each candidate. A menu of other reports are

available addressing various decision points throughout the life cycle of the

employee, including the onboarding process, training programs,

management, coaching and career pathing.
