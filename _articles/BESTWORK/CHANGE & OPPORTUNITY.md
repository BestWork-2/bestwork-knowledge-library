Change & Opportunities in an Agile DATA World

In the 1930’s, nuclear magnetic resonance was discovered. It was used to

study the chemical composition of compounds. Forty years later, the first

medical application was achieved. It was not until the 1980’s that the first

medical use MRI was available. Now physicians use about thirty million

MRI scans each year. The level of information they provide has

dramatically changed the practice of medicine. Physicians using MRI

information made better decisions. They saw solutions that were not visible

before.

The history of the evolution of the MRI offers an interesting parallel to that

of agile DATA. Scientists knew about magnetic resonance for over forty

years before it was applied to medical practice. Even then, it was another

forty years before doctors could use it with their patients. It took another

thirty years to reach the level of common practice, where the idea of

surgery or even diagnosis in many cases is unthinkable without the data

from an MRI.

In the 1950’s, psychologists had determined that human beings had hard-

wired personality traits that remained stable, not changing to any extent

over their lifetime. The psychological world also agreed on a model of those

traits, which they called “the Big Five” along with a set of subtraits called

facets. At that time, these traits were measured with psychometric

instruments that were rather complex, being designed for clinical use. They

often included assessments of psychoses and neuroses, which were

meaningful to clinicians, but which precluded their use in the business

world. These instruments also required analysis and interpretation by

experts, severely limiting the use of the information. This was also the age

of paper assessments and no Internet.

In the 1970’s, there were several attempts to adapt clinical instruments for

the business world. The clinical elements were removed and a simple

profiling methodology was introduced to eliminate the need for

interpretation. Top performers were tested and the data was compiled to

create templates or profiles. Job candidates were then tested and their

results compared to the profiles, showing a percentage match to that

profile. Using the data with this methodology usually benefited the hiring

Unfortunately, it locked the potential of the data into a limited application

In the 1990’s, several events occurred that enabled an experienced

management consultant to work closely with several of the top

psychometric instruments, ultimately becoming the principal advisor on

their use. For four years, he had a laboratory of about 400 consultants and

distributors working with the tools in hundreds of different businesses. With

that knowledge and through his own consulting practice, he explored a

wide range of applying the data to executive team analytics and work group

inventories. For a consultant, the data showed the underlying structure or

topology of the people. It was the same as a physician seeing an MRI. The

only catch was that it still required special training or the help of an expert

to realize the value of the data.

In the 2000’s, he set out to create a system that would effectively eliminate

the need for such help or training. After five years of development, followed

by four years of market testing and input from users, BestWork was

launched. It is based on the concept of agile DATA. The psychometric

instruments that drive the DATA are the most sophisticated in the world.

They produce accurate and reliable measurement of how individuals think,

learn and behave. The DATA is used to enhance countless business

decisions that depend upon the thinking, learning and behavior of

employees. There are hundreds of outputs, with the DATA being specifically

purposed for a particular audience and a particular decision point. These

range from screening and selection to interviewing, onboarding and

training. The DATA has outputs for management, coaching, career pathing,

troubleshooting, team engineering, mentoring and more. The DATA

becomes the operating system for human resources, being used

throughout the life cycle of the employees.

The DATA is aggregated into work groups for further analyses, such

comparing the operational strengths of a team with the operational

strategies of that team. Lead With DATA is a special set of analytics

designed for consultants. Objective measurements are available on

leadership, creativity, customer centricity, change agility, cultural topology

and much more. If it depend upon human thinking and behavior, the DATA

can report on it. It is not tied to any methodology, but it is applicable across

a wide variety of consulting models. BASF once aired television

commercials that proclaimed, “BASF doesn’t make race cars. We make

race cars go faster.” DATA does that for consulting firms by giving talented

and experienced professionals a higher level of information and information

that more intimately links them to their clients.

The ease of use and accessibility of agile DATA shatters the current

paradigms of hiring, training and management. It does this with a simple,

pragmatic change in how human behavior is understood.

•If you know an individual cannot sell solutions, do you interview

them?

•If you know an individual cannot effectively manage the actions of

others, do you put them into a position that requires that.

•If you know exactly how each member of a team can contribute to the

work of that team and what the obstacles will be, what can you do?

•If you know exactly what the strategic strengths of an executive team

are, how does that affect the operating strategies of that team?

•If a company is considering a merger/acquisition, would it make

sense to know how viable the strategy for combining the companies

will be?

•What if you understood leadership on a much deeper level than a

generalized concept of “leadership?” Would it then make sense to

match the type of leadership to roles in which that type of leadership

is needed?

•What if, instead of surveying employees’ opinions on culture, you

could actually measure the aggregate personalities of the various

elements of the enterprise in objective terms?

•What if before proposing any intervention, an inventory of the

strengths of the work group was acquired and that was the basis for

initial discussions with the prospective client?

Incredible changes are already taking place. Early adopters have

discovered that agile DATA has the potential for being a strong and

sustainable competitive edge in the human capital management world.

There is so much more to discover.
