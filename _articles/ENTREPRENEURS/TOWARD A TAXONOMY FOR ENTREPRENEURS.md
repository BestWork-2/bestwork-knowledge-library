Toward a Taxonomy of Entrepreneurs

Too often, entrepreneurship has been touted as a universal elixir for

economic health. Entrepreneurship for individuals; for frustrated corporate

salarymen; for daring students; for women returning to the workforce; and

for anyone with a new idea. Large corporations have been warned to

create entrepreneurship centers within themselves or risk missing the

waves of innovation rolling through the business world. Unfortunately, such

broad applications of the term is like promoting vitamins as an important

additive to anyone’s diet. It does not explain what vitamins to take or what

to expect when they are taken. Entrepreneurship is indeed an important

option for many people, but a deeper and more specific definition is

necessary if the true potential of entrepreneurship is to be realized.

What drives someone to become an entrepreneur? There are different

drivers, and each calls for different factors to be available if the

entrepreneurial enterprise is to be successful.

The Drivers: Necessity - The simplest driver is necessity. A job is lost; a financial need occurs; or some event triggers a need for money.

•A musician is playing in the Underground, hoping for tips from

the passing commuters.

•An old woman is selling flowers to passersby on the street.

•A student becomes a nanny.

Autonomy - This offers independence and not having to work for

someone else. It is the belief that one can thrive away from the

rules and structure of an employer.

•An accountant leaves a large professional services firm to

open their own office.

•A consultant leaves a group practice to go solo.

•A sous chef leaves one restaurant to open their own

restaurant.

•A corporate executive leaves the large company and buys a

franchise.

An Idea - A person has an idea or some innovation that they believe

can support a business, and they set out to develop that idea and

bring it to the market.

Opportunity - A person notices that a particular product or service

is not available in a certain area, creating an opportunity which they

can fill.

•A pizza delivery service is started at a lake resort area which

has few restaurant options nearby.

•A rickshaw service is started at a cruise port where ships dock

almost a kilometer from the gate at which buses let off the

passengers.

Ownership - A person wants to have their own business in which

they can build equity and secure their family financially.

•Franchises are ideal for this.

•They may also purchase an existing business which can use

their previous knowledge and experience.

DATA & the Drivers

Each of these Drivers creates a different type of entrepreneurial business.

Each type requires a somewhat different set of critical strengths and

abilities. There are similarities but recognizing the differences is essential

for optimizing the potential of the business. In many cases, it can be

essential for survival.
