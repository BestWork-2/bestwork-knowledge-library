Everything Old Is Not New Again

If a friend of yours showed up for their match at the tennis tournament with

a wooden racquet, my guess is that you would think it was some sort of a

joke, intended to throw off their opponent. If they said they had always

played with that racquet and really intended to use it in the match because

it still worked fine, you might first acknowledge the sentiment. Then you

would explain that new racquets had bigger sweet spots, more powerful

frames and used advanced strings that delivered more effective spin.

Playing with the old familiar wooden racquet was a major disadvantage

when competing with the other players who were better equipped. Similar

reactions would occur if you saw a business purchasing typewriters or

Commodore 64 computers. Imagine your family’s reaction if you brought

home a black & white console television. The fact is, that in a technological

world, everything old is not new again. Wooden racquets are interesting,

and nostalgic, and they look good on the wall in the den. That is further

than you can go with the tv, typewriters and the Commodore 64’s.

Almost anyone can recognize these anachronisms, where the technology

and science is so far behind the times. The design of the objects

themselves reveals the age of the science and technology that supports

them. The same is definitely not true in the world of personality

assessments. They all come out of the printer looking brand new, with the

latest graphics and page design. The science and technology is hidden.

Yet, the effectiveness of such instruments rests solidly on: •The psychological theories and science it is based upon •The psychometric science and technology that is used to collect the data •The science and technology that was used to develop, calibrate and validate the instrument •The technology and methodology that is used to deliver the outputs.

None of this is visible to most HR professionals, business executives,

trainers or consultants. Not recognizing that technology has moved on, they

continue to make serious business decisions with products based on

science from the 1920’s and 1940’s, when even the tools from the 1970’s

are outdated. Tube TV’s did not stop working. Typewriters still type. And
