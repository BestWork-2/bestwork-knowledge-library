The Differences Between Measurements & Surveys

Hiring decisions are some of the most critical in the business world. Trying

ensure better decisions, businesses look at a wide range of information

from many different sources. Conceptually this makes sense, but

unfortunately, the quality of the information varies greatly. It is like finding

a box of foreign currency. Do you want 10 dollars, 40,000 rials or 140,000

rupiahs? Some hiring information is objective, accurate and reliable. Most

is subjective and undependable. Sometimes it is helpful and sometimes it

is misleading.

The most accurate information is obtained using 7th Generation

psychometric instruments, such as BestWork DATA. These measure hard-

wired personality traits and cognitive abilities that are necessary to support

the job behaviors needed for the specific job in question. As measured

with these tools, these traits and abilities do not change with training or

coaching, therefore it makes sense to know what they are early in the

selection process.

Skills and experience are important for many jobs. Sometimes these can

be determined with certification or demonstrations, but with many jobs

this is impossible. The good news is that if the hard-wired traits and

cognitive abilities of a candidate are right for the job, they can be trained

to acquire the skills needed.

Business also want to know about other things such as honesty,

dependability, cultural fit, interests, emotional intelligence and a myriad of

others. These cannot be measured. They are surveyed. Surveys can be

helpful but the information is not necessarily reliable or accurate.

The information from such surveys is always questionable to some extent

because of that.

What about interviews? They are subjective and their effectiveness is

dependent on how well trained the interviewer and the interviewee are.

Interviews are also affected by the differences between the interviewer and

the candidate in terms of their own hard-wired traits and abilities.
