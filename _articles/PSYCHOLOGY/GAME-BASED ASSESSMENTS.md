A Perspective on Game-based Assessments

Video games are fun and engaging. The concept of using video gaming to

assess behaviors is also engaging. It sounds innovative and progressive.

Unfortunately, it is not the most robust solution in terms of the information it

provides nor for its cost. There are four reasons for this: 1.Gaming behavior itself is based on hard-wired personality traits and cognitive abilities that are easily and accurately measured with current instruments. Drawing conclusions about personality and cognitive ability from gaming behaviors is a kind of reverse engineering. It is similar to asking a candidate to make a sales presentation and then from observing that, trying to discern their personality and reasoning abilities. It is not that it will not reveal something of that information, but it is of a much lower order of specificity and accuracy. The same is true of the information derived from gaming behavior.

2.Psychometric science has seen amazing advances in the last ten

years. Item construction has become incredibly precise at tapping

into personality traits. The constructs used have been refined to

target the key factors of job behaviors. The time to do this has

shrunk dramatically. Cognitive measures have experienced the same

quantum leaps in accuracy and precision. Game-based

assessments wrap this precise and specific data within a fabric of

artificial behaviors and hypothetical deduction. From the standpoint

of the quality of the data, it is a step backwards.

3.The methodology used by most game-based assessment programs

is the old profiling model common among older assessment products

from the 80’s & 90’s. Creating well-founded profiles is a complex and

expensive project. Typically, the process uses profiles or templates
