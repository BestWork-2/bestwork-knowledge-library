## Page 1

Does Your Test Go Clunk?

There is a story of a rather frustrated man, who after years and years of driving the same vehicle, took the old car to the mechanic. The man complained to the mechanic that sometimes, “The car just goes Clunk!” The mechanic stood there thoughtfully for a few minutes, considering the problem. Then with calm and professional assurance, he explained, “That model does that.” Older assessment products do not really go “Clunk”, but they do have characteristics that can be equally frustrating to their users. Just as the driver of the “clunker car” gets used to the rattles and mechanical glitches of the car he drives every day, some assessment users come to accept high costs, confusing terminology, and extensive training as a necessary part of using good psychological instruments.

Others use old fashioned products such as DISC and Myers-Briggs, which are

interesting and fun, but decades behind current psychology and psychometric

methodology. This happens because information on this subject is hard to find.

Most HR professionals have never been educated on the quality issues for

comparing assessment tools.

Historically, the thousands of assessment products have fallen into two distinctive groups: the accurate, technically sound ones and the less accurate, technically outdated ones. The sound ones typically required almost an hour to complete; cost well over $100 apiece; used psychological terminology that was unknown to the average executive; and generally required the users to undergo substantial training or certification in order to be qualified to use it with any degree of effectiveness. Some of the best ones needed expert consultants to work with the company to, as my grandmother said about the pecans, really get the goody out of the assessment.

Most businesses are “sold” the assessments they are using, by test salespeople

who usually have little knowledge of the other 80,000 assessments in the market,

but who are convinced that their product is the best. They may not know the

importance of cognitive measurement or why normative instruments are the best

for most purposes. They may not understand the realities of validation or how the latest instruments can actually strengthen legal compliance and drive a more meaningful diversity program.

Using my twenty-five years of specializing in the business applications of

psychometric assessments, I am launching a Website that is designed to

educate laypeople on the qualitative differences in assessments. There is no

selling…just information. It is not complete but it is dynamic and more will be

added as we hear questions. I invite you to visit www.aboutassessments.com.
