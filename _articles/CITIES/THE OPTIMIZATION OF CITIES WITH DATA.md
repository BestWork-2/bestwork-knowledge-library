The Optimization of Cities with DATA

It is ironic that the most powerful resource within a city is too often the

source of its greatest challenges. People start and operate businesses.

They build homes and establish communities. They raise families and

educate their children. In the best cases, the businesses thrive; the

communities are vibrant; and the children grow up to add more life to the

city. In a complex world, this is often not the case.

About half of the SME’s in most cities fail. This creates a steady current of

unemployment and a sense of uncertainty in the families of those affected.

The digital revolution, artificial intelligence and other economic events

cause disruptions in the business foundation of the cities. Workers must be

retrained and find new roles in a changing world. These training programs

have a poor record of success, despite the apparent need and the moneys

spent on such programs. The very best education or training that is

mismatched to the strengths and abilities of the participants is wasted. It is

the same as training a Sumo wrestler to become a pole vaulter. Even if

they acquire the knowledge, they are not able to perform the sport at a

competitive level.

Cities must recruit new businesses or industries to replace those that are

struggling. What businesses to attract and how to attract them are major

questions. Cities frequently compete with one another for the businesses.

Education has its own challenges. Many educational programs are not

preparing students for today’s business environment. In some countries,

there is huge unemployment at the same time as the businesses in those

countries are decrying the lack of qualified workers to fill millions of jobs.

The majority of students in universities lack a clear direction to a viable

career. In too many places, the education is simply not connected to

employability.

The foundation of every city is its people. The foundation of the people is

the set of hard-wired personality traits and cognitive abilities that determine

how each person thinks, learns and behaves. The people never lose these

strengths and abilities. They remain constant throughout a lifetime of

challenges. They are the foundation upon which any education or training

must be built. What does this mean to the cities?

Imagine that a city has the ability to deploy DATA applications in several

ways. DATA refers specifically to a set of proprietary applications which use

an online experience to accurately and reliably measure an individual’s

hard-wired personality traits and cognitive abilities. This DATA can then be

used to describe how that individual will perform any specific job behaviors.

It is an easily understood language for connecting the strengths and

abilities of people to the behavioral requirements for a particular job. This is

critical because for every person there is a finite number of jobs that do not

fit their set of strengths and abilities, and these are best to avoid if possible.

Even when the circumstances require a person to work at that job for some

period of time, knowing and understand the difficulties makes it easier. The

good news is that there is an infinite number of jobs that do match the set

of strengths and abilities of each person. These are jobs which that person

can perform successfully, some adequately and some exceptionally.

When the strengths and abilities of people are recognized and

acknowledged as being hard-wired, and not adaptable to any type of job or

any type of training, cities will discover that they have much simpler and

more practical variables to address many of their issues.

•SME’s - Any SME within the city can use the DATA applications to

ensure that its salespeople can sell; its managers can manage; and its

employees can provide the services or produce the products efficiently

and with quality. The DATA enables them to use the talents of their

employees more effectively. Communication improves. Sales increase.

Customer service is better. In actual practice, DATA has done this with

hundreds of companies. Revenue has increased along with profitability.

•Education 1 - Secondary schools provide DATA-based guidance on the

most promising career directions, including college and non-college

directions. Consequently, students from that city complete their

education with few detours and major changes. More importantly, the

DATA provided a direct connection to the students’ employability. The

job choices can even be focused on those jobs that are most beneficial

to the city.

•Education 2 - Vocational schools and colleges use DATA-based

guidance applications to direct students to the most promising courses

of study leading to jobs that match their strengths and abilities. Course

•Employment - DATA-based applications can enable people to match

their strengths and abilities to hundreds of types of jobs available in the

city. DATA-based matching lets people look beyond what they have

always done for new kinds of jobs. If they have the strengths and

abilities, it is possible to learn the necessary skills and knowledge.

Employers can also search for people based on specific job capabilities

and behaviors, knowing with confidence that the person can do the job

even before the interviews. This creates a dynamic employer-job seeker

system that facilitates a robust employment climate.

•City Services - Specific DATA applications have been developed for

police departments, identifying candidates who are most likely to

successfully complete academy training. The DATA then matches them

with the field training officers that will be the most effective mentors for

them. Similar applications are available for fire departments, waste

management, power and other city services.

•Workforce Training - People seeking training in the city’s workforce

programs use a simple online experience to determine their job-related

strengths and abilities. This DATA provides a clear picture of what type

of training will be most effective for that individual in terms of leading

them to sustainable employment. This opens an entirely different

perspective on re-skilling. The starting point becomes what can the

person do with their hard-wired set of strengths and abilities instead of

what they have done in the past that does not exist anymore. This is

especially powerful with people with disabilities. One workforce group,

using DATA for several years, renamed their disabilities program as a

capabilities program, focusing on what the participants CAN do rather

than what their disabilities were.

•Strategic Macro-Analytics - The many DATA-based applications

described above all contribute that DATA into what is effectively an

inventory of the human resources of the city. It becomes clear what job

training will be most beneficial for the city. It becomes clear what type of

workforce they can offer to potential new businesses which they wish to

The impact of these DATA applications is game changing for cities. DATA

applications within businesses have produced significant revenue

increases for businesses with long histories of profitability. When applied

across hundreds of businesses in a city, the collective benefits increase tax

revenue, create new jobs and spur additional business growth. Giving clear

guidance to hundreds of students has long lasting benefits for the schools

and for the families. DATA makes the workforce of the city more agile and

adaptive to changes in the economy or the job environment. All of the

ensuing DATA enables the city itself to make sound decisions and spend

resources more effectively. The means to do this already exists. It is time to

reach far beyond traditional thinking and what has always been done. In

this time of change and innovation, it is time to bring the power of

innovation to life in each and every city with DATA.
