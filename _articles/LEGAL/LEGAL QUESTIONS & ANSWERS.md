Legal Questions & Answers

Questions about the legal uses of pre-employment testing were fairly common in

the 90’s with about 30% of companies using some form of assessments. Today

over 80% of companies have used or are using some type of testing. It is still

prudent to understand the issues that are present in hiring processes that employ testing. I have been an expert witness on the legal applications of testing and have written several pieces on the subject. Perhaps this one can clarify the actual facts.

BestWork does not provide selection procedures nor do we have any control over

the selection procedures chosen by our clients. We provide objective and non-

discriminatory information on specific job-related behaviors. This information is used in a client’s selection process at various points in conjunction with other information to make decisions on each candidate’s viability in a particular role.

There have been no cases on any assessment instruments in the last decade,

although several have focused on the misuse of assessments. There have been none

involving BestWork.

Actually, the use of BestWork is an excellent way to document non-discriminatory

hiring practices. The greatest vulnerability to discrimination is found in the

interviewing process, and next in the review of work experience, resumés and

references. Personal opinions and biases along with misused information are

fraught with hazards, whereas the BestWork information is objective and tied to

actual job elements.

The validity question is a good question to ask, but the answer is somewhat

complex. In the psychometric world, test validity refers to the degree to which

evidence and theory support the interpretations of the test scores. There are

multiple methods for assessing validity, and each offers a different perspective on the question. In most cases, the question is driven by two concerns: Is this a quality product? Does the use of it comply with legal standards?

One of the co-authors of the American Psychological Association’s Standards for

Educational and Psychological Testing led the BestWork team in developing the

instruments. This ensured the highest compliance with those standards. He is also the co-author of the standard university textbook on personality assessment. It is not surprising that high standards of reliability and validity were achieved under his direction.

A clear understanding of the legal issues involved in assessing job candidates and employees was fundamental to the design and development of the instruments.

Avoiding any adverse impact was paramount. Once the instruments were

completed, a leading authority on the business applications of assessment

technology was engaged to create the reports and user processes for BestWork. He

had extensive experience advising companies on using effective selection tools

while ensuring compliance with legal standards. His participation helped ensure

that BestWork’s information and outputs were in compliance with current legal

statutes and guidelines.

The actual application of BestWork avoids the most troublesome element of most

assessment methodologies, which is the question of using success profiles,

benchmarks or other models as a point of comparison with job candidates. This

comparison to a model, which is sold as being simple and easy, is actually the crux of any legal argument charging discrimination. The question hinges on how the profile was developed. The world of variables virtually ensures that the model has issues with opinions, lack of reliable metrics, too small a sample, and the dynamic nature of some jobs.

BestWork simply describes how each candidate will perform the key elements of

the job in question. It leaves the decision as to whether that is acceptable to the client.
