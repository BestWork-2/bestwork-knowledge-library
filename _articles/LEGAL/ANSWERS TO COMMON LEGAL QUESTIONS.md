ANSWERS TO COMMON LEGAL QUESTIONS

- What are the legal challenges that this system has withstood?

There has not been a substantial challenge to any assessment instrument

in over several decades. A normal element of our practice is the audit of a

selection system in terms of compliance, economy, and effectiveness. I

made the point in my book that prior to 5th Generation assessments, there

were no instruments that had been designed specifically for business use.

Consequently, many businesses, intent on using psychometrics to select

the right people for the right job, chose instruments designed for clinical

use or used outdated products that incorporated methodologies that had

long been discarded. The two primary examples of this are the Target

stores use of the Minnesota Multiphasic Personality Inventory, a clinical

diagnostic instrument, and Duke Power’s use of a crystallized intelligence

test to discriminate against minorities. Even today, it is not uncommon for

me to discover First Generation tools such as the DISC being used to make

hiring decisions.

It is important in selecting instruments for a hiring process, to use only Fifth, Sixth or Seventh Generation tools, since those were created specifically for that purpose. These instruments have incorporated critical elements such as diversity of race, age, sex, and national origin into the psychometric studies that preceded the genesis of the instrument. Similarly, the occupations of the sample population were diversified from senior level executives to blue collar workers; the same is true for educational differences. These top level instruments ensure that the population of the studies reflects the population of the work force in the United States.

Another critical point in using assessments for selection and screening is

that the construct, or scales of the instruments are clearly work-related.

BestWork DATA wisely chose to measure only scales that are: a)

universally accepted by the psychological community as being measurable

and b) core traits and abilities that are fundamental to all occupations. The

research over the last ten years has clearly shown that these traits and

abilities are critical performance factors in all but the most rote tasks. The

more knowledge is involved, the more critical the factors are to

performance.

The most vulnerable point for any business in its hiring process is the

consistency of application. Some points of concern are inconsistent

interviewing, lack of documentation, and inconsistent flow of process.

Rather than being a source of exposure, Fifth Generation and later

Generations of assessments are the only way in which a business can

document objective and nondiscriminatory hiring practices. Assessments

represent the only truly objective element in the system. BestWork DATA

goes beyond that by standardizing much of the interview process as well.

Not only are the questions much more focused on the candidate’s

performance issues, but the same questions are asked of each candidate

that scored the same.

It is important to remember that excluding those candidates that are a poor

match to the demands of the job is vital to two parties. The company

benefits, of course, but equally rewarding, is preventing a hard working

person from taking a job that offers little chance of success.

To bring this point into perspective in a legal sense, The Uniform Guidelines

on Employee Selection Procedures (1994) states that a selection system

must provide fair and equal employment opportunities to all people. It

proceeds to state that Testing may be used:

- To screen out those applicants who are not likely to be able to perform

the job successfully

- To group applicants in accordance with the likelihood of their

successful performance

- To rank applicants, selecting those with the highest scores for

employment.
