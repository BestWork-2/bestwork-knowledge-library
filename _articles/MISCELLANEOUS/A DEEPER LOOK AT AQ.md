A Deeper Look at the Adaptability Quotient

This is the new “emotional intelligence”. The concept is appealing to

anyone. It makes sense. It even appears to be helpful and in a limited way

it is. BestWork measures hard-wired behavioral traits with state of the art

psychometric instruments. The measurements are accurate. AQ is

assessed with a simple survey. My score on one was 40 indicating that I

was “on my way to being adaptable.” I am sure workshops could be

cobbled together to discuss AQ and engage in some exercises to stretch

the participants’ AQ. The same is true for emotional intelligence and a

handful of similar interesting concepts.

But consider what actually determines what is AQ. It rests on a foundation

of behavioral traits and cognitive abilities. For example, what level of

general reasoning and fluid intelligence would support a high level of AQ?

What level of AQ would be expected for an individual in the middle of the

bell curve in general reasoning?

Creativity depends upon two primary traits/abilities : a personality trait of

openness to new experiences and ideas and the fast processing of

information to explore new ideas. Lacking either of these limits anyone’s

AQ.

Emotional resilience is hard-wired and measurable in BestWork. Without

that trait a discussion of AQ is moot.

Each of the elements of AQ rely on specific behavioral traits and cognitive

abilities which are easily measurable. Once measured, that DATA can be

applied in countless ways that are practical and real. As separate DATA

points, they offer clear translation into what is possible and what is not on

an individual basis or work groups or an enterprise. The generalized nature
