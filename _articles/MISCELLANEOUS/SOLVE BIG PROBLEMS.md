Solving BIG Problems

When I was originally thinking about what kind of business to start, one of my role model/mentors advised me,

Solve big problems.

I liked that. Solving big problems pays better than solving small problems, and big problems are more challenging, which means for me

more fun. Plus, there is no shortage of big problems. I have pursued that objective for a couple of decades with some success. Along the way in the past few years, I have observed the proliferation of would-be problem solving Internet businesses that pop up faster than pickpockets on the subway. (I used that metaphor because both are after your money with little or nothing given return.)

Big problems attract two types of attention:

Problem feeders

Problem solvers

The first group cannot really solve the problems, but they offer a flood of pseudo solutions to a population desperate for help. The hallmarks of this group are pseudo-scientific claims and flashy marketing. They can make quick money for a short time but the problems remain.

The second group takes more time and puts in more work, but they can really solve the problems. The marketing is not as flashy, and the real science is a bit harder to understand. When people finally discover that the solutions work, they are happy to pay for them.

I am especially familiar with two industries that are rife with problem feeders:

Pre-employment assessments

College and career direction assessments for students

Both of these categories feature a majority of vendors with outdated, simplistic surveys, whose primary attraction was the low cost or no cost to use it. The idea of producing accurate or reliable information was unimportant. It just had to look good for an audience with real problems and no real knowledge of the quality differences in psychometric tests. Not only are these outdated from a technical standpoint, but most were never intended for this purpose.

The result of this is that job seekers fail to get the right job; sometimes get the wrong job and fail at it; students pursue the wrong majors at a hideous cost to their parents and companies needing productive employees bring in good people who cannot do the job in question. Imagine how it feels to get poor, incomplete or even wrong directions when you are searching for an address (without GPS.) Bad assessments give people poor, incomplete or even wrong directions for their lives.

Your ability to achieve your full potential is directly related to how specifically you understand your strengths and how they relate to your goals.
