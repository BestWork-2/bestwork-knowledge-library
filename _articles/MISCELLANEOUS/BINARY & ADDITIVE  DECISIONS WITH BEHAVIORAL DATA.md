Binary & Additive Decisions with Behavioral DATA

In exploring the factors affecting business decision making when faced

with change, the initial step was to unbundle the decisions into two

types: binary and additive. The following definitions will clarify the

connotations being used in this context.

binary - a business decision between two alternatives that are essentially

opposite

Examples : •Computerize business operations or not •Have a website or not additive - a feature or capability which is added to the business to effect a desired change in performance Examples : •Upgrade software •Video features added to website Businesses continually face the challenge of change. Advances in technology present both challenges and opportunities that affect the performance of the enterprise. These challenges are of two types : binary and additive. Binary challenges offer only one solution which will preserve the life of the business. In the 80’s, businesses were faced with the imperative of computerizing their operations. Failing to do so would mean doom for that business. It was a binary change…there was no option. The Internet introduced several binary changes. Websites are essential for virtually any type of business. Email addresses are critical bits of identity and mobile numbers are not far behind.

Additive changes offer performance improvements for the business. It

may be an upgrade to a software program. It may be introducing videos

and animation to the business website. The critical difference between

additive and binary changes is that the additive ones are optional. The

performance improvement is desirable but the life of the business does

not depend on it. When faced with a binary change, the business must

make that change to remain competitive.

Early adopters recognize binary changes before others. Their computer

integrations had been established and were working smoothly when their

competitors were just realizing that computers were a binary change,

not an additive change. Similarly, their websites were well known and

and a major part of their marketing program before their competitors had

even surfed the web.

BestWork is a binary change in talent acquisition and talent

management which is easily seen when compared to the results

produced with current methodologies. Whatever the mix of assessments,

interviewing techniques, recruiting strategies, AI applications and other

things, employers fail to hire the talent they expected only about half the

time. The most conservative surveys paint a dismal picture of the state of

talent acquisition effectiveness.

•75% of employers feel that they have hired the wrong person.

•One bad hire costs about $17,000 on average.

•80% of turnover is caused by bad hiring decisions.

•2/3 of workers say they have accepted a job that proved to be a

poor fit.

•50% of those workers quit within 6 months.

•Recruiting at all levels, from hourly employees to managers and

even executives has a failure rate of 50%.

If this was happening on a swim team, half of the swimmers would have

drowned. The coaches would be spending most of their time trying to

drownproof the team instead of trying to win the meet.

While it is hard to imagine why this is acceptable, there is no doubt it is

being accepted. The employment screening services market is worth

$12.6 billion. That is excellent pay for services that do not work half the

time. Imagine if cars ran every other day. Computers could flip a digital

coin to see if they would work. Mobile phones randomly answered half

the calls and made half the calls. One does not even want to imagine a

healthcare facility with a 50% failure rate,

One of the principle causes of this failure rate is the reliance on

assessment products whose quality and effectiveness is drastically

overstated. According to SHRM, 83% of businesses use pre-employment

assessment tools. This means that the information provided by these

assessments is a major contributor to the 50% failure rate. Interviews

have proven to add little to the success rate and some interviewing

strategies actually increased the problems.

As grim as this is, it represents one side of the binary options. A

business can continue to use their same selection process, or they can

employ the methodologies used by other firms in their market.
