## Page 1

From Concept to Real Change

I have read many well-intended business books on how people are the key

to the future of business. For decades, advances in technology have led

the way with people adjusting to change after change. Technology will

continue to change but the changes will be more incremental. The

competitive advantages that businesses seek will now only be found in

human capital…the people. However, while the concepts of the potential

within people can be discussed and applauded, that potential can only be

realized by understanding each individual’s unique set of hard-wired

strengths and abilities.

Businesses are no different than baseball teams. The team consists of

baseball players, and while the coaches can talk about “winning” and

“playing hard,” ultimately that must translate into what pitch is thrown to

what batter at what point in the game. That decision depends on the

individual capabilities of that particular pitcher. The degree to which the

coach knows those capabilities is the degree to which they can execute the

necessary strategies to “win the game.”

Business consultants and writers promote positive concepts such as: collaboration (One third of the population cannot do that.)

innovation (One third of the population is not innovative.)

strategic thinking (Half of the population cannot do that.)

leadership (Leading whom? In what situation?)

transformation (One third of the population struggles with change.)

communication (With whom? How often? What result?)
